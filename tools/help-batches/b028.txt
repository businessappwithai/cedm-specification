@ LegalMatter
s: A unit of legal work that groups parties, counsel, documents, obligations, filings, issues, costs and outcomes.
b: Legal work rarely consists of a single event. A matter is the file that holds everything about one legal issue, so counsel, cost and outcome can be tracked together.
u: Opened when a legal issue arises; filled as filings, documents and cases are added; closed when resolved; read by legal counsel and management.
c: A matter contains LegalCases and refers to the documents, filings and obligations that belong to it.
l: Finalised evidence and effective history are preserved; settlements and corrections are explicit.
x: The dispute with a former supplier over a delivery failure, with a mediation, a court case and a settlement.
a legalMatterId
s: The identity of the matter.
u: Generated on opening; cases, costs and documents refer to it.
a occurredAt
s: When the matter was opened or the event that gave rise to it.
u: Set at opening; used to order matters and compute limitation periods.

@ Load
s: A grouping of shipments, consignments, handling units or containers that are assigned together to one transport execution.
b: Freight moves in loads, not in single orders. Grouping what travels together lets the carrier, the dock and the customer see one planned movement and track it as such.
u: Created by transport planning; filled as shipments are assigned; handed to a carrier; read by dispatch and the warehouse.
c: A load groups Shipments and Consignments under one carrier movement.
l: Changes and retries preserve history and do not duplicate commercial, physical or financial effects.
x: A full trailer carrying eight pallets for four customers from the central warehouse to a regional depot.
a loadId
s: The identity of the load.
u: Generated on creation; the shipments and the carrier refer to it.
a code
s: The load number printed on the loading list.
u: Allocated by transport planning; used by drivers and the dock.

@ Location
s: A physical or logical place where resources, activities, stock, services or organisational operations are situated.
b: Almost everything in a business happens somewhere. The location is the shared reference for that: sites, warehouses, stores, offices, ports and even virtual places, arranged in a hierarchy so addresses, stock and assets can all point to the same place.
u: Created when a place becomes relevant; arranged under parent locations; referenced by assets, stock, facilities and addresses; read by logistics, facilities and reporting.
c: A location may sit within a parent Location with children beneath it, has an Address and may belong to an Organization.
l: A location is planned, becomes active when in use, may be inactive for a time, and is closed or retired when no longer used. History and references remain valid.
x: The Rotterdam distribution centre, a warehouse location within the Netherlands site, with its own address.
a locationId
s: The identity of the location, which stays the same when its name or code changes.
u: Generated on creation; assets, stock and documents refer to the location through it.
a code
s: The code of the place in the organisation's site list, such as NL-RTM-DC1.
u: Unique; assigned by the administrator and used in integrations and labels.
a name
s: The name people use for the place.
u: Shown in lists, maps and documents.
a locationType
s: What kind of place it is.
u: Chosen at creation; decides which processes can use the location.
v SITE: A geographic site that may contain several buildings.
v WAREHOUSE: A building or area for storing goods.
v STORE: A retail outlet.
v OFFICE: A place where office work is done.
v FACTORY: A place where goods are made.
v YARD: An open area for storing or staging equipment or containers.
v PORT: A harbour or terminal.
v DEPOT: A base for vehicles and equipment.
v VIRTUAL: A logical place with no physical presence, such as an online store.
v OTHER: Any other kind of place.
a status
s: Whether the place is in use.
u: Set by the administrator; only ACTIVE locations are offered for new assignments.
v PLANNED: Expected but not yet in use.
v ACTIVE: In use.
v INACTIVE: Temporarily not used.
v CLOSED: Closed down.
v RETIRED: Removed from use altogether. A final state.
a addressId
s: The postal address of the location.
u: Chosen from the address list; used for deliveries, mapping and tax.
a parentLocationId
s: The place that contains this one, such as the site that holds a warehouse.
u: Set to build the hierarchy; a top-level place has none.
a createdAt
s: When the location was created.
u: Set by the system.
a updatedAt
s: When the location last changed.
u: Set by the system.
r parentLocation
s: The place that contains this one.
u: Chosen to place the location in the hierarchy.
n: At most one parent; a top-level site has none.
w: Lets reports and stock roll up from the smaller place to the larger.
r childLocations
s: The places directly within this one.
u: Read to expand a site into its buildings and areas.
n: Any number of children, including none.
w: Reflects the hierarchy from the other side.
r address
s: The address of the location.
u: Chosen when the place has a postal address.
n: At most one address; virtual places have none.
w: Provides delivery and map position.
r organization

u: Set where operation is clear.
n: At most one operating organisation.
w: Determines responsibility and reporting.

@ LossEvent
s: A record of realised financial, operational, physical, legal or other loss that can be attributed to a risk or incident.
b: Risk management is judged by outcomes. A loss event records what was actually lost, so risk estimates can be tested against reality and insurance and recovery claims can be supported.
u: Recorded after an incident when the loss is known; read by risk managers, finance and insurers.
c: A loss event is linked to the risk it materialised and to the incident that caused it.
l: Finalised evidence and effective history are preserved; reassessments and recoveries are separate, explicit records.
x: A 120,000 loss from a warehouse fire, linked to the fire incident and the property damage risk.
a lossEventId
s: The identity of the loss event.
u: Generated on recording.
a occurredAt
s: When the loss was suffered.
u: Set from the incident; used to place the loss in a reporting period.
