@ Charge
s: A billable amount produced from a subscription, usage, service, product, fee or adjustment rule, before it is settled.
b: A charge is the line between something the customer received and something they will be invoiced for. Holding it as a record lets billing check, adjust or cancel amounts before they become part of an invoice.
u: Generated by billing runs and manual adjustments; reviewed before invoicing; read when a customer queries what they were billed for.
c: A charge belongs to the Customer it is billed to. It never replaces the invoice, payment or ledger entries that follow from it.
l: A charge is drafted, becomes active when ready to bill, completes when it has been invoiced and cancelled if it should not be billed. It does not rewrite cash, accounting or tax records.
x: A monthly platform fee of 49.00 for March, generated by the billing cycle for a subscriber account.
a chargeId
s: The identity of the charge.
u: Generated on creation; invoice lines and adjustments refer to the charge through it.
a status
s: Where the charge stands before it is billed.
u: Moved by the billing run and by billing staff; only ACTIVE charges are picked up by invoicing.
v DRAFT: Generated but not yet checked; excluded from invoicing.
v ACTIVE: Confirmed and waiting to be invoiced.
v COMPLETED: Invoiced; the charge has done its job.
v CANCELLED: Withdrawn and will not be billed.
r customer
s: The customer who is billed for the charge.
u: Set when the charge is generated; invoices are assembled per customer.
n: At most one customer; a charge for an internal cost centre may have none.
w: Decides which invoice the charge lands on and whose credit exposure it affects.

@ ChemicalBatch
s: A traceable batch of chemical or process-industry material that was produced, received or processed under controlled conditions.
b: In process industries the batch is the unit of quality and traceability. If a batch turns out to be faulty, the batch record shows where it was made, what was tested and where it went.
u: Created when a batch is planned, produced or received; updated as it is tested, released or consumed; read by quality, production and recall processes.
c: A batch is of one Product, is stored at a Location and is judged through QualityInspections.
l: A batch is planned, then held in quarantine until it passes quality. Released batches can be consumed, quarantined again if a problem appears, or expire. Rejected, expired and fully consumed batches are final.
x: Batch B-2026-0417 of 2,000 litres of solvent, made on 3 March, quarantined until its purity test passed and released on 5 March.
a chemicalBatchId
s: The identity of the batch record.
u: Generated on creation; inspections, stock movements and recalls refer to it.
a batchNumber
s: The lot number assigned to the batch, printed on labels and certificates.
u: Allocated at production or receipt; unique, and the key by which the batch is traced forwards and backwards.
a manufactureDate
s: The date the batch was produced.
u: Entered at production; the start of the batch's shelf life.
a expiryDate
s: The date after which the batch must not be used.
u: Set from the product's shelf life; stock past it is blocked. It must not be before the manufacture date.
a status
s: Whether the batch may be used.
u: Set by quality and production; only RELEASED batches may be issued to production or sold.
v PLANNED: Scheduled to be produced or received; no material exists yet.
v QUARANTINED: Present but held until quality checks pass; not usable.
v RELEASED: Passed quality checks and available for use.
v REJECTED: Failed quality checks; must not be used.
v EXPIRED: Past its expiry date; no longer usable.
v CONSUMED: Fully used up.
r product
s: The product of which this is a batch.
u: Set at creation; determines the specification the batch is tested against.
n: Exactly one product; a batch is of one product.
w: Gives the batch its specification and shelf life.
r location
s: Where the batch is currently stored.
u: Updated as the batch moves; used for picking and inventory checks.
n: At most one current location; empty while the batch is not yet stored.
w: Supports stock visibility and recall.
r qualityInspections
s: The inspections carried out on the batch.
u: Read when deciding release and when investigating a quality complaint.
n: Any number; a new batch may have none yet.
w: Release depends on passing inspections.

@ City
a cityId

u: Generated when the city is loaded; addresses refer to the city through it.
r country
n: Exactly one country; a city belongs to a single country.

@ ClaimLine
a claimLineId
s: The identity of the claim line.
r claim
n: Exactly one claim; a line is one component of a single claim.
r coverage
n: Exactly one coverage; each line is assessed against the coverage under which it is claimed.

@ Class
a classId
s: The identity of the class offering.
a classCode
s: The code by which the offering is known in timetables and registration, such as BIO101-A-2026F.
a name
s: The name of the offering as shown to students.
a startDate
s: The first day of the offering.
a endDate
s: The last day of the offering.
u: Entered at scheduling; must not be earlier than the start date.
a status
v DRAFT: Being planned; not yet visible to students.
v OPEN: Open for enrolment.
v IN_PROGRESS: Teaching has started.
v COMPLETED: Finished; results are final and kept as delivery evidence.
v CANCELLED: Called off before completion.
r course
n: Exactly one course; a class is always an offering of a single course.
r enrollments
n: Any number, up to the class's capacity.
r assessments
n: Any number of assessments, each belonging to the class.

@ Classification
s: A reusable classification scheme or category value that classifies records consistently instead of by free-text labels.
b: Free-text categories drift: one person writes "Premium", another "premium". A classification gives the organisation one agreed list, so reporting, rules and searches group things the same way.
u: Maintained by data stewards; chosen by users on the records they classify; read by reports and rules that group by category.
c: Many kinds of record can point at a classification, which is what lets different domains share one vocabulary.
l: Code and identity stay stable, and history is never silently rewritten when a classification is renamed or retired.
x: Customer segment "Enterprise", used to group accounts in sales reports and pricing rules.
a classificationId
s: The identity of the classification value.
u: Generated on creation; classified records refer to it.
a code
s: The stable code of the classification, such as ENTERPRISE.
u: Assigned by the steward; used in rules and integrations, so it should not change.
a name
s: The display name of the classification.
u: Shown in pick-lists and reports; can be reworded without changing the code.

@ Collateral
s: An asset, guarantee or other value pledged to secure a loan, facility or other credit exposure.
b: Collateral is what the lender can fall back on if the borrower fails to repay. Recording it, with the exposure it secures, lets risk and credit teams see how well covered a loan is.
u: Registered when security is taken; revalued over time; read by credit risk and collections.
c: Collateral secures a BankLoan or other facility; its value offsets the exposure.
l: Collateral is taken, held, and released when the exposure is repaid. Corrections are recorded explicitly, not by editing past valuations.
x: A commercial property pledged against a five-year business loan, valued at 400,000.
a collateralId
s: The identity of the collateral record.
u: Generated on creation; valuations and releases refer to it.
a effectiveAt
s: When the pledge took effect.
u: Set at registration; the loan is considered secured from this moment.
r bankLoan
s: The loan the collateral secures.
u: Set when the pledge is registered.
n: At most one loan; collateral may secure another type of facility instead.
w: Links the security to the exposure it covers.

@ Compensation
s: An effective-dated agreement about how an employment is paid, separate from calculating and settling that pay.
b: What someone is paid is a commitment that changes over time, such as a raise or a new pay basis. Keeping it as its own dated record shows what was agreed at any point and keeps it separate from the payroll runs that apply it.
u: Created at hire and whenever pay changes; read by payroll when it calculates pay and by HR when it reviews compensation.
c: Compensation belongs to one Employment, is expressed in a Currency, may be tied to a Position, and is applied by Payroll runs.
l: A compensation is drafted, becomes active on its start date, and is superseded when a newer arrangement replaces it or cancelled if never applied. Compensation used by completed payroll is never rewritten.
x: Annual salary of 72,000 EUR for a senior analyst from 1 January, superseding the previous 68,000 arrangement.
a compensationId
s: The identity of the compensation arrangement.
u: Generated on creation; payroll results refer to it so pay can be explained.
a compensationCode
s: The reference for the arrangement, used in HR approvals and payroll queries.
u: Unique; assigned when the arrangement is created.
a basis
s: The unit in which the amount is expressed.
u: Chosen when the arrangement is created; payroll uses it to turn the amount into pay for a period.
v HOURLY: The amount is paid per hour worked.
v DAILY: The amount is paid per day worked.
v MONTHLY: The amount is a monthly salary.
v ANNUAL: The amount is an annual salary.
v FIXED: A fixed sum, such as a one-off or contract fee, not tied to time.
v OTHER: Any other basis defined by the contract.
a amount
s: The agreed amount per pay basis.
u: Entered by HR; not negative. Payroll multiplies or prorates it by the basis.
a effectiveFrom
s: The date from which the arrangement applies.
u: Set when the arrangement is agreed; payroll for earlier periods uses the arrangement then in force.
a effectiveTo
s: The last date on which the arrangement applies.
u: Set when a later arrangement takes over; must not be before the start date.
a status
v DRAFT: Being prepared; not yet used by payroll.
v ACTIVE: In force for the employment.
v SUPERSEDED: Replaced by a later arrangement; kept as history.
v CANCELLED: Withdrawn before it took effect.
r employment
n: Exactly one employment; a compensation arrangement belongs to a single employment.
r currency
n: Exactly one currency, so the amount is unambiguous.
