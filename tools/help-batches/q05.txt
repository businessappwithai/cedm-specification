@ LegalEntity
a incorporationDate
s: The day the company or organisation was legally formed, as shown on its certificate of incorporation.
u: Copied from the official register when the entity is set up; used for age checks, filings and group structure charts.
c: It can never fall after the dissolution date.
a dissolutionDate
s: The day the legal entity formally ceased to exist and was removed from the register.
u: Entered by company secretarial when a dissolution is confirmed; it stays empty for entities that still exist.
c: Only dissolved entities carry a date here.
a status
v ACTIVE: Registered, in good standing and free to trade, sign contracts and issue invoices.
v DORMANT: Still registered but not trading, so it holds no live business activity.
v IN_LIQUIDATION: Being wound up by a liquidator; no new business should be started in its name.
v DISSOLVED: Struck off the register and gone for good; no new contracts or invoices may use it.
@ LegalMatter
a title
s: A short headline telling lawyers and the business what the dispute, advice request or transaction concerns.
u: Typed by the legal team at intake and shown in matter lists, searches and cost reports.
c: Read together with the matter number to pick the right matter out of a list.
a description
s: The background of the matter, the facts so far and what the business hopes to achieve through legal help.
u: Written at intake by the requesting lawyer and extended as the matter develops; it may contain privileged advice.
c: Access is limited to the legal team and those they name.
a openedDate
s: The day the legal team formally accepted the matter and began work on it.
u: Set at intake, normally when the request is approved; it starts the clock for ageing and cost reporting.
c: It cannot be later than the closing date.
a closedDate
s: The day the legal work was finished and the matter was closed in the register.
u: Recorded by the responsible lawyer together with the final outcome and last counsel invoice.
c: Empty while the matter is open or on hold.
a spendToDate
s: The total legal fees and expenses charged against the matter so far.
u: Updated as outside counsel invoices are approved; the legal team compares it with the budget each month.
c: Going over the budget needs approval from the matter owner.
a status
s: Shows whether the matter is still being worked on, paused, or finished.
u: Changed by the responsible lawyer as work starts, stalls or ends; closed matters need an outcome recorded.
c: Closing a matter locks in its dates and final spend.
@ Load
a plannedLoadingAt
s: The date and time the dock team expects to start loading the vehicle.
u: Booked in the dock schedule when the load is planned; yard staff use it to assign doors and labour.
c: It cannot be later than the planned departure.
a dispatchedAt
s: The date and time the vehicle carrying the load actually left the site.
u: Recorded by gate staff or the driver check-out; carriers and customers use it to judge on-time delivery.
c: Filled only once the load has been dispatched.
a unloadedAt
s: The date and time the receiving site finished unloading the load at its destination.
u: Confirmed by the receiving site on arrival; it closes the transport leg and starts goods receipt checks.
c: Filled only after the load is unloaded.
a status
s: Tells dock and transport teams where the load is in its journey, from planning to unloading.
u: Moved forward by the dock as loading starts and the vehicle leaves, then by the receiving site on unloading.
c: Only planned loads may still be changed; once loading begins, changes need the dock supervisor.
v PLANNED: Shipments are still being assigned and the load can be freely changed.
v LOADING: The vehicle is at the dock and goods are being put on board.
v DISPATCHED: The vehicle has left the site and the load is in transit.
v UNLOADED: The receiving site has taken the goods off the vehicle and the transport is complete.
v CANCELLED: The load was called off before dispatch and its shipments return to planning.
r origin
s: The place the load starts from, where the vehicle is loaded and departs.
u: Chosen when the load is planned, usually a warehouse or distribution site, and used for route and dock planning.
n: At most one origin per load, though many loads may start from the same location.
w: Fixes the start of the transport leg so carriers, dock schedules and distance calculations know where to begin.
@ LossEvent
a lossNumber
s: The unique reference by which risk managers, auditors and insurers quote this loss.
u: Assigned automatically when the loss is recorded and never reused, even if the loss is later withdrawn.
c: No two loss events share a number.
a title
s: A one-line description of what was lost, such as stolen stock or a failed payment run.
u: Entered when the loss is recorded and shown in loss reports and dashboards.
c: Read alongside the loss number to identify the event.
a description
s: A narrative explaining how the loss came about and what it affected.
u: Written by the risk analyst when recording the loss and updated as the assessment finds more.
c: The linked incident holds the detailed facts of what happened.
a occurredDate
s: The day the event that caused the loss took place.
u: Taken from the linked incident or investigation; it can differ from the day the loss was found.
c: It cannot be later than the discovery date.
a discoveredDate
s: The day someone first realised the loss had happened and reported it.
u: Recorded when the loss is reported; period-end loss reporting groups events by this date.
c: Required, since losses are reported by discovery rather than occurrence.
a grossLoss
s: The full value lost before any insurance payout, recovery or write-back reduces it.
u: Estimated when the loss is first recorded and confirmed by the risk team during assessment.
c: Held in the loss's own currency.
a netLoss
s: What the loss really cost the business once recoveries have been taken off.
u: Worked out as the gross loss less the amount recovered; risk and finance reports use this figure.
c: Changes whenever new recoveries are booked.
a status
s: Shows how far the loss has been assessed and whether its amounts can still change.
u: Moved on by the risk team as estimates are confirmed and recoveries settle.
c: Closed losses are frozen and no longer edited.
v IDENTIFIED: The loss is known and recorded, but its amounts are still estimates.
v ASSESSED: The amounts are confirmed, although further recoveries may still arrive.
v CLOSED: Final amounts are recorded and the loss is frozen for reporting.
v WITHDRAWN: The loss was recorded by mistake and is excluded from all totals.
@ MaterialIssue
a costAmount
s: The value of the components taken from stock and charged to the production order.
u: Calculated when the issue is posted and booked as material cost on the work order.
c: Priced at the product's current cost rather than its purchase price.
@ Message
a direction
s: Says whether the business received this message from another system or sent it out.
u: Set by the integration layer when the message is created; support staff filter on it when tracing a problem.
c: Received messages are turned into business records, and sent ones come from them.
v INBOUND: Received from an external system and waiting to be turned into business records.
v OUTBOUND: Created by this application and sent to an external system.
a sentAt
s: The time the message left the application, or arrived if it was received from outside.
u: Stamped by the integration layer on transmission; support staff compare it with the acknowledgement time.
c: Message lists are sorted by this time.
a attemptCount
s: The number of times the system has tried to deliver or process this message.
u: Raised by one on every try, including failed ones; operators watch it to spot messages that keep failing.
c: Once it reaches the configured maximum the message is moved to the dead-letter queue.
@ Obligation
a obligationNumber
s: The unique reference people use when chasing or reporting on this duty.
u: Assigned when the obligation is recorded and quoted in reminders, breach notices and compliance reports.
c: No two obligations share a number.
a obligationType
s: The kind of duty owed, such as paying money, delivering goods or keeping information secret.
u: Chosen when the obligation is recorded; it determines who monitors it and which evidence is expected.
c: Payment duties usually also appear in the accounts.
v PAYMENT: A duty to pay a sum of money by an agreed date.
v DELIVERY: A duty to hand over goods, a service or a document.
v REPORTING: A duty to report, notify or file information with someone.
v PERFORMANCE: A duty to carry out an action or reach an agreed standard.
v CONFIDENTIALITY: A duty to keep specified information secret from others.
v RESTRICTION: A duty not to do something, such as competing or soliciting staff.
a frequency
s: How often the duty has to be performed again, from a single event to every year.
u: Chosen when recording the obligation; after each performance it drives the calculation of the next due date.
c: One-off and ongoing duties have no next occurrence.
v ONCE: Performed a single time and then finished.
v MONTHLY: Falls due again every month after each performance.
v QUARTERLY: Falls due again every three months after each performance.
v ANNUAL: Falls due again once a year after each performance.
v ONGOING: Applies continuously, as with confidentiality, so it never has a due date.
a metDate
s: The day the duty was last performed in full.
u: Recorded by the owner alongside the evidence, such as a payment receipt or signed delivery note.
c: Filled only once the obligation has been met.
a status
v PENDING: Not yet due, or due but not yet performed.
v MET: The duty has been performed and the evidence recorded.
v WAIVED: The party owed the duty has formally released the other from it.
v BREACHED: The duty was not performed by its due date.
@ OrganizationMembership
a startDate
s: The day the person or party officially became a member of the organisation.
u: Recorded when they join; it is used for access rights, headcount reports and membership history.
c: It cannot be later than the end date.
a endDate
s: The day the membership stops, after which the person is no longer part of the organisation.
u: Recorded when the member leaves and left empty while the membership is open-ended.
c: Access tied to the membership should end with this date.
@ Packaging

v CASE: A shipping case or carton holding several units.
v PALLET: A full pallet of cases ready to be moved by forklift.
a lengthCm

u: Entered from the packaging specification and used by warehouse and transport planners to fit stock into locations and vehicles.
c: Measured on the outside of the package.
a widthCm
s: The outer width of one package, measured in centimetres.
u: Entered from the packaging specification and used by warehouse and transport planners to fit stock into locations and vehicles.
c: Measured on the outside of the package.
a heightCm
s: The outer height of one package, measured in centimetres.
u: Entered from the packaging specification and used by warehouse and transport planners to plan stacking and vehicle loading.
c: Measured on the outside of the package.
@ Packing
a packedAt
s: The date and time the packer finished and closed the packed handling unit.
u: Recorded when the unit is closed and labelled, ready for the shipping area.
c: Filled only once packing is done.
@ PartyRelationship
a relationshipType
s: Describes how the first party stands towards the second, such as subsidiary, employee or guarantor.
u: Chosen when the link is recorded; credit, compliance and group reporting use it to interpret the connection.
c: Read from the first party to the second.
v SUBSIDIARY_OF: The first party is a subsidiary owned by the second.
v BRANCH_OF: The first party is a branch operating as part of the second.
v EMPLOYEE_OF: The first party works for the second as an employee.
v AGENT_OF: The first party acts as an agent on behalf of the second.
v GUARANTOR_FOR: The first party guarantees the second party's obligations.
v BENEFICIAL_OWNER_OF: The first party ultimately owns or controls the second.
v HOUSEHOLD_MEMBER_OF: The first party lives in the same household as the second.
v OTHER: Any other kind of link, explained in the relationship name.
a validFrom
s: The first day on which the relationship between the two parties holds.
u: Recorded when it is known, for example from a contract or register entry.
c: It cannot be later than the valid-to date.
a validTo
s: The last day on which the relationship between the two parties holds.
u: Recorded when the relationship ends, for example on resignation or sale; empty while it still applies.
c: Empty while the relationship continues.
a status
s: Says whether the relationship between the two parties is still in force today.
u: Set to ended by the person maintaining the record when the relationship stops.
c: Ended relationships stay for history.
v ACTIVE: The relationship is in force today.
v ENDED: The relationship has stopped but is kept for history.
@ PaymentInstruction
a amount
s: The sum the bank is being told to pay to the payee.
u: Taken from the approved invoices or entered by hand when the instruction is created, and checked at approval.
c: In the paying account's currency unless a foreign payment is ordered.
a payeeAccount
s: The payee's bank account number or IBAN to which the money will be sent.
u: Copied from the payee's verified bank details; a change must be verified before it is used.
c: Wrong details send money to the wrong place.
a paymentMethod
s: The way the bank is asked to move the money, from standard transfer to cheque.
u: Chosen by treasury when creating the instruction; it decides the bank file format, the fee and the speed.
c: Urgent and cross-border payments cost more.
v CREDIT_TRANSFER: A standard bank transfer settled in the normal cycle.
v URGENT_TRANSFER: A same-day, high-value transfer that carries a higher fee.
v CROSS_BORDER: An international transfer to an account abroad, possibly with currency conversion.
v CHEQUE: A cheque printed and issued by the bank.
v DIRECT_DEBIT: A debit collected from the payer's account under an existing mandate.
a approvedAt
s: The date and time the final approver authorised the instruction for release to the bank.
u: Stamped on the last approval; treasury cannot send the instruction without it.
c: Required before sending.
a sentAt
s: The date and time the instruction was transmitted to the bank.
u: Stamped by the bank interface on transmission and used when reconciling with the bank statement.
c: Filled only after sending.
a bankStatusMessage
s: The status or rejection text the bank returned for this instruction.
u: Copied from the bank's status report so treasury can see why a payment failed or was held.
c: Explains rejections and holds.
@ Picking
a pickedQuantity
s: The number of units the picker actually took from the storage location.
u: Confirmed by the picker on the handheld device; it is compared with the requested quantity.
c: A figure below the requested quantity means a short pick.
a pickedAt
s: The date and time the picker confirmed this pick on the handheld device.
u: Recorded automatically on confirmation; supervisors use it to measure pick rates and delays.
c: Filled only once picked.
@ Pricing
a validFrom
s: The first day on which this price may be used for quotes and orders.
u: Price changes are scheduled by setting this date; price determination ignores the price before it.
c: It cannot be later than the valid-to date.
a validTo
s: The last day on which this price may be used for quotes and orders.
u: Left empty when the price runs until it is replaced; the price expires after this date.
c: Prices for the same conditions should not overlap.
a status
v DRAFT: Still being set up; it is not yet approved and price determination does not find it.
v APPROVED: Approved and waiting for its start date.
v ACTIVE: In force now and found by price determination.
v EXPIRED: Past its end date or replaced, so it no longer applies.
@ ProcessDefinition
a definitionVersion
s: The version number of this process definition, rising with each published change.
u: Increased by the process designer when a revised definition is published.
c: Running instances keep the version they started on.
a definitionFormat
s: The notation in which the process steps are written, which tells the engine how to run them.
u: Chosen when the definition is created and read together with the definition content.
c: Changing it means rewriting the content.
v BPMN: Business Process Model and Notation, a flow diagram with branches and parallel paths.
v STATE_MACHINE: A set of states and the allowed moves between them.
v CHECKLIST: An ordered list of steps without any branching.
a publishedAt
s: The date and time this version was released for use by new process instances.
u: Stamped when the designer publishes the version; drafts have no value.
c: Present only once published.
@ ProcessInstance
a instanceNumber
s: The unique reference for one run of a process, quoted when tracking or investigating it.
u: Assigned by the engine when the instance starts and used in monitoring screens and audit trails.
c: No two instances share a number.
a definitionVersion
s: The version of the process definition that this instance is following.
u: Fixed by the engine when the instance starts, so the run is audited against the rules it began with.
c: Publishing a later version does not change a running instance.
a startedAt
s: The date and time the process run began.
u: Set automatically by the engine when the instance starts; durations and overdue checks measure from it.
c: Durations are measured from this time.
a completedAt
s: The date and time the process run finished, whether it succeeded or was cancelled.
u: Set by the engine when the last step ends or the run is stopped; empty while still running.
c: Present only once finished.
a dueAt
s: The deadline by which the process run should be completed.
u: Calculated at start from the definition's target duration so owners can see which runs are late.
c: Running instances past this time are overdue.
@ ProductLifecycle

u: Recorded when the change is approved, so audits can show who signed off on a product's eligibility.
n: At most one approver per record, though one person may approve many changes.
w: Gives accountability for lifecycle changes that affect customers, such as withdrawing a product from sale.
@ ProductVariant
a gtin
s: The global barcode number that identifies this exact variant.
u: Entered from the manufacturer or GS1 registration and scanned in stores and warehouses to find the variant.
c: Each variant has its own number, not shared with its sibling variants.
@ ProductionReceipt
a scrapQuantity
s: The quantity of output rejected at receipt because it failed quality checks.
u: Recorded by the production team at receipt for yield measurement; scrap is never added to stock.
c: Excluded from the accepted quantity.
@ ProductionRecord
a scrapQuantity
s: The quantity produced but thrown away because it was unusable.
u: Entered by the production team for yield and waste measures.
c: Not counted in the good output quantity.
a shift
s: The working shift, such as Night or Day, during which the output was produced.
u: Entered by the supervisor so output and scrap can be compared between shifts.
c: Free text such as "Night".
@ ProfitCenter
a name
s: The descriptive name of the profit centre as shown on management reports.
u: Entered when controlling creates the profit centre and shown beside its code on reports and in pick lists.
c: Read together with the code to pick the right centre.
a status
s: Says whether new transactions may still be assigned to this profit centre.
u: Moved on by controlling when a centre is opened or retired.
c: Closed profit centres are not offered in selection lists.
v ACTIVE: Open and available when assigning revenue and costs.
v CLOSED: Retired and kept only so past reports stay readable.
@ ProjectPhase
a plannedStart
s: The date on which work in this phase is scheduled to begin.
u: Set in the project plan by the project manager and revised when the schedule changes.
c: It cannot be later than the planned end.
a plannedEnd
s: The date by which this phase is scheduled to be finished.
u: Set in the project plan; progress reports measure delay by comparing the actual end against it.
c: Delays are measured against this date.
a actualStart
s: The date on which work in this phase really began.
u: Recorded by the project manager when the phase starts, which may differ from the plan.
c: Present once the phase is active.
a actualEnd
s: The date on which this phase was really finished.
u: Recorded by the project manager when the phase is completed or signed off.
c: Present once the phase is completed.
a budget
s: The amount of money approved for the work done in this phase.
u: Set in the project plan and compared with the costs booked to its tasks.
c: Held in the project's currency.
