@ Farm
a isOrganicCertified
s: Says whether the farm currently holds an organic certification from a recognised certifying body.
u: Ticked by the farm manager once certification is granted and cleared if it lapses; it decides whether produce may be sold and labelled as organic.
c: Read together with the certification date, which shows when the status was granted.
a certificationDate
s: The date the farm's current organic or quality certification was granted by the certifying body.
u: Entered from the certificate; auditors use it to trace produce back to a certified period and to plan renewal. It stays empty for uncertified farms.
@ Filing
a filingNumber
s: The company's own internal reference for this filing, quoted in deadline calendars and correspondence.
u: Unique; assigned when the filing is first recorded, before the authority has issued any reference of its own.
c: The authority's reference is kept separately as the acknowledgement reference once the filing is lodged.
a title
s: A short description of what is being filed, such as "Annual accounts 2023" or "Change of registered office".
u: Written by the person preparing the filing and shown in deadline calendars, so that compliance staff can recognise it without opening the record.
a feeAmount
s: The fee the authority charged for accepting the filing, in the currency it was paid in.
u: Entered once the fee is paid and used for expense tracking and budgeting of regulatory costs. Many filings carry no fee and leave it blank.
a status
s: Shows how far the filing has progressed, from being identified as required to the authority's answer.
u: Moved on by the compliance team as the papers are prepared, lodged and answered.
c: A required filing that is still unsubmitted after its due date is treated as overdue.
v REQUIRED: Known to be needed, but nobody has started preparing it.
v IN_PREPARATION: The papers are being drawn up and have not yet been lodged with the authority.
v SUBMITTED: Lodged with the authority, which has not yet answered.
v ACCEPTED: The authority has accepted the filing; the obligation is met.
v REJECTED: Refused by the authority, so a corrected filing is needed.
v WITHDRAWN: Found not to be needed, or withdrawn after it was lodged.
@ Finding
a findingNumber
s: The reference by which the finding is cited in the audit report and in follow-up with management.
u: Unique; assigned when the auditor raises the finding and then used in tracking lists and committee reports.
a title
s: A one-line statement of the weakness found, readable without opening the full finding.
u: Written by the auditor and shown in finding lists, dashboards and the issued report, so it should name the control or process affected.
a condition
s: The facts of what the auditor found, including what was observed and the evidence behind it.
u: Written by the auditor and agreed with management for accuracy before the report is issued; it should describe the situation, not the cause.
a rootCause
s: The underlying reason the weakness arose, as agreed with management after analysis.
u: The agreed action should address this cause; without it, fixes tend to treat only the symptoms and the same finding returns.
a severity
s: How serious the weakness is, rated by the auditor against the organisation's risk scale.
u: Chosen when the finding is raised; it sets the due date for remediation, decides who is told, and feeds the audit's overall opinion.
v LOW: A minor weakness where an improvement is recommended but no urgent action is needed.
v MEDIUM: A weakness to correct within the normal planning cycle.
v HIGH: A significant weakness to correct promptly.
v CRITICAL: A serious failure needing immediate action and senior attention.
a agreedAction
s: The corrective action that management committed to take in response to the finding.
u: Written with the action owner after the finding is raised; the owner reports progress against it and the auditor later checks that it worked.
a remediatedDate
s: The date the action owner reported that the agreed action had been completed.
u: Recorded by the owner; it starts the auditor's verification, which follows before the finding can be closed.
a verifiedDate
s: The date the auditor confirmed that the fix is in place and actually works.
u: Recorded on verification and required to close the finding. It cannot be earlier than the remediated date.
a status
s: Shows where the finding is in its life, from being raised through remediation to closure.
u: Moved on by the action owner as work progresses, while only the auditor can close a finding after verifying the fix.
v OPEN: Raised, but management has not yet agreed an action.
v IN_REMEDIATION: An action has been agreed and is under way.
v REMEDIATED: The owner reports the action complete and awaits verification.
v CLOSED: Verified by the auditor, so nothing more is required.
v RISK_ACCEPTED: Management decided not to act, with the approval required to carry the risk.
@ Flight
a actualDepartureAt
s: The time the aircraft actually left the departure airport, as opposed to the scheduled time.
u: Recorded by operations at pushback or take-off; it is empty until the flight departs and drives delay reporting.
a actualArrivalAt
s: The time the aircraft actually reached the destination, as opposed to the scheduled arrival.
u: Recorded by operations on landing or arrival at the gate; it is empty until the flight arrives and is used to measure punctuality.
@ Forecast
a name
s: A descriptive name for the forecast, such as "Q3 rolling cash outlook", that tells readers its purpose.
u: Chosen by finance when the forecast is started and shown in reports and in lists of forecast versions.
a horizonStart
s: The first day covered by the forecast, from which the projected periods run.
u: Set when the forecast is started, typically the day after the as-of date; it may not be later than the horizon end.
a forecastType
s: Says what kind of measures the forecast projects, such as cash, sales or headcount.
u: Chosen when the forecast is created and decides which measures it holds.
c: Cash forecasts are usually prepared weekly and financial forecasts monthly.
v FINANCIAL: Projects revenue, costs and profit over the horizon.
v CASH: Projects receipts, payments and cash balances over the horizon.
v SALES: Projects sales volumes and revenue by product or customer.
v HEADCOUNT: Projects the number of people and the personnel cost they bring.
a totalRevenue
s: The total revenue the forecast expects over its whole horizon.
u: Summed from the forecast detail lines rather than typed in; management compares it with the approved budget.
a totalCost
s: The total cost the forecast expects over its whole horizon.
u: Summed from the forecast detail lines rather than typed in; management compares it with the approved budget.
a assumptions
s: The key assumptions behind the numbers, such as growth rates, exchange rates and timing of large receipts.
u: Written by finance and read by management when reviewing; it should explain what changed since the previous forecast.
a status
s: Shows where the forecast is in review, from a draft to the current approved outlook.
u: Moved on by its author and the reviewers as it is submitted, approved and eventually replaced.
c: Only one approved forecast per scenario is current at a time.
v DRAFT: Still being worked on by its author; no one has been asked to review it and it is not yet the current outlook.
v SUBMITTED: Sent for review and waiting for the approver's decision.
v APPROVED: Accepted by the approver and in use as the current outlook.
v SUPERSEDED: Replaced by a later approved forecast and kept only for history.
@ ForeignExchangeTransaction
a tradeDate
s: The day the dealer and the bank agreed the currency exchange.
u: Recorded by the treasury dealer when the deal is struck; it may not be later than the value date and starts the exposure.
a settledAt
s: The moment both currency payments of the deal were completed.
u: Recorded by treasury operations when the bank confirms settlement. It stays empty while the deal is open.
a status
s: Shows how far the deal has progressed from being dealt to being settled or cancelled.
u: Moved on by treasury as the bank confirms the deal and both payments are made.
c: Only unsettled deals count in the open currency position.
v DEALT: Agreed with the bank, but not yet matched with its confirmation.
v CONFIRMED: Matched with the bank's confirmation and awaiting the value date.
v SETTLED: Both currencies were exchanged on the value date.
v CANCELLED: Cancelled by agreement with the bank before settlement.
@ FreightCharge
a invoicedAmount
s: The amount the carrier actually billed for the transport service.
u: Entered from the carrier's invoice and compared with the expected amount during freight audit; differences may be disputed.
a disputeReason
s: The explanation of why the charge is being disputed with the carrier, such as a wrong rate or a surcharge not agreed.
u: Written by the freight auditor when the invoiced amount differs from the expected one, and filled only on disputed charges.
r carrier
s: Names the carrier that is charging for the transport service.
u: Taken from the shipment's consignment, so the auditor knows whose rates and invoice to check the charge against.
n: Each charge belongs to at most one carrier, and may have none yet.
w: The carrier's agreed rates give the expected amount that freight audit compares with the invoice.
@ Fulfillment
a status
v RELEASED: Released to the warehouse with stock reserved, but work has not yet started.
v PICKING: Being picked from stock by warehouse staff.
v PACKED: Picked and packed, and waiting for the carrier to collect it.
v SHIPPED: Handed over to the carrier for delivery.
v DELIVERED: Received by the customer.
v ON_HOLD: Stopped by a problem that someone must resolve before work continues.
v CANCELLED: Withdrawn because the order is no longer needed; no further work is done.
a shippedAt
s: The time the goods were handed over to the carrier.
u: Recorded on dispatch and used for customer notifications and for judging whether the promised date will be met.
a deliveredAt
s: The time the customer received the goods.
u: Taken from the carrier's proof of delivery; it is empty until delivery and closes the fulfillment.
@ HandlingUnit
a heightCm
s: The height of the packed unit, in centimetres, including any pallet base.
u: Measured at packing or taken from the packaging type; used for loading plans, stacking limits and carrier quotes.
a status
v OPEN: Still being filled; goods can be added.
v CLOSED: Packed and labelled, and not to be reopened without unpacking.
v SHIPPED: Has left the warehouse on its way to the destination.
v RECEIVED: Arrived at its destination and was booked in.
v DISMANTLED: Unpacked, so its contents are loose again.
@ HealthcareOrder
a orderType
s: Says what kind of clinical order this is, such as a laboratory test, an imaging study or a medication.
u: Chosen by the ordering clinician; it decides which department receives the order.
c: Medication orders follow the prescribing rules.
v LABORATORY: A laboratory test on a patient sample.
v IMAGING: An imaging study such as an X-ray or scan.
v MEDICATION: A medicine to be given or dispensed to the patient.
v PROCEDURE: A procedure to be performed on the patient.
v REFERRAL: A referral to another service or specialist.
v NURSING: A nursing intervention carried out by the ward team.
a priority
v ROUTINE: To be carried out in the normal course of the department's work.
v URGENT: To be done sooner than routine orders, ahead of them in the queue.
v STAT: To be carried out immediately, ahead of all other work.
a instructions
s: Directions for whoever carries out the order, such as fasting requirements or which side to image.
u: Written by the ordering clinician when signing and displayed to the performing department.
a orderedAt
s: The date and time the clinician placed the order.
u: Recorded on signing; turnaround times are measured from it, and it cannot be later than the completion time.
a completedAt
s: The date and time the order was carried out.
u: Recorded by the performing department, and empty until the work is done; it ends the turnaround measurement.
@ Hotel
a starRating
s: The official star classification of the hotel, from one to five stars.
u: Entered from the hotel's classification certificate and shown to guests on booking channels.
a checkInTime
s: The earliest time of day guests may take their room, written as hours and minutes such as 15:00.
u: Local time at the hotel; shown on booking confirmations so guests know when to arrive.
a checkOutTime
s: The latest time of day guests must vacate their room, written as hours and minutes such as 11:00.
u: Local time at the hotel; shown on confirmations and used by housekeeping to plan room turnover.
a roomCount

u: Entered by the hotel manager and used as the capacity figure in occupancy and revenue reports; it should match the rooms recorded.
a phone

u: Entered in international format with the country code, and printed on booking confirmations.
a email
s: The email address guests and partners use to contact the hotel.
u: Usually the reception desk or reservations mailbox; printed on booking confirmations.
@ Incident
a incidentNumber
s: The reference by which the incident is tracked, reported and discussed during the response.
u: Unique; assigned automatically when the incident is reported and quoted in all communications.
a title
s: A one-line statement of what happened, readable at a glance in lists.
u: Written by the reporter and shown in incident lists, dashboards and reports; it should name the system or place affected.
a description
s: A factual account of what happened, as reported and later completed by investigators.
u: Written by the reporter and extended during investigation; keep it to facts, because causes belong in the root cause.
a containedAt
s: The time the incident stopped getting worse or spreading.
u: Recorded by the responders when containment is confirmed; it is used to measure response performance and stays empty until then.
a resolvedAt
s: The time normal operation was restored after the incident.
u: Recorded by the responders when service or safety is back to normal; it ends the duration used in incident statistics.
a rootCause
s: Why the incident happened, established after investigation.
u: Written after investigation and required to close the incident. Recurring causes are taken up by problem management.
a status
s: Shows how far the response has progressed, from being reported through containment to closure.
u: Moved on by the coordinator and responders as the incident is triaged, contained and resolved.
c: A closed incident must have a root cause.
v REPORTED: Reported and waiting for triage.
v INVESTIGATING: Triaged, and responders are working on it.
v CONTAINED: The effect has been stopped from spreading.
v RESOLVED: Normal operation is restored.
v CLOSED: Root cause and lessons are recorded and nothing more is to be done.
v INVALID: Turned out not to be an incident after all.
@ IntegrationEndpoint
a name
s: A readable name for the endpoint, such as "Bank payment file upload", that people recognise in monitoring.
u: Chosen by the integration team when registering the endpoint and shown on monitoring screens next to its code.
a authenticationMethod
v NONE: No authentication, allowed only for internal endpoints carrying nothing sensitive.
v API_KEY: A secret key sent with each request.
v OAUTH2: Access tokens obtained from an authorization server.
v CERTIFICATE: A client certificate presented to prove the application's identity, which must be renewed before it expires.
v SSH_KEY: An SSH key pair, typically for file transfer.
v BASIC: A user name and password.
a status
v PLANNED: Being built or tested, with no live traffic.
v ACTIVE: In live use and receiving messages.
v SUSPENDED: Traffic stopped for now, with messages waiting.
v RETIRED: Decommissioned and no longer used.
@ Interest
a dayCountBasis
s: The convention for counting days when working out interest for the period.
u: Taken from the agreement; the same rate gives a different amount under each convention.
v ACT_360: Actual days elapsed divided by 360.
v ACT_365: Actual days elapsed divided by 365.
v THIRTY_360: Each month counted as 30 days, divided by 360.
a periodEnd
s: The last day of the period for which interest is calculated.
u: Set by the interest run; periods for the same balance must not overlap.
a postedAt
s: The date and time the interest was booked to the accounts.
u: Recorded when finance posts the calculation; empty while the interest is only calculated.
a status
s: Shows whether the calculated interest has been booked to the accounts.
u: Moved on by finance when it posts, cancels or reverses a calculation.
c: Only posted interest appears in the accounts.
v CALCULATED: Worked out and waiting to be checked and posted.
v POSTED: Booked to the accounts.
v CANCELLED: Found wrong before posting and replaced by a new calculation.
v REVERSED: Posted, then undone by an opposite entry.
@ InventoryAdjustment
a approvedAt
s: The date and time a manager approved the stock correction.
u: Recorded on approval; adjustments above a value limit need approval before they can be posted, and it is empty until then.
a status
s: Shows how far the stock correction has progressed from request to posting.
u: Moved on by the requester, the approver and the posting step.
c: Only posted adjustments change the stock.
v REQUESTED: Asked for and waiting for approval.
v APPROVED: Approved and waiting to be posted.
v POSTED: Applied, so stock and accounts are updated.

@ InventoryCount

s: The counted quantity minus the quantity the system holds for the location.
u: Calculated for review by the supervisor; large differences are recounted before being adjusted. It may be negative.
a countType
s: Says why the count was made, whether routine, year-end or after a problem.
u: Chosen when the count is planned; it decides who counts and when.
c: Annual counts freeze stock movements while they run.
v CYCLE: A routine count of some locations on a rolling schedule.
v ANNUAL: The full year-end count of all stock.
v SPOT: An unplanned count made after a problem was suspected.
a status
s: Shows how far the count has progressed from planning to approval.
u: Moved on by the counter and the supervisor.
c: Differences are adjusted only from approved counts.
v PLANNED: Scheduled, but not yet counted.
v COUNTED: Counted, with a difference to review.
v RECOUNT: A recount was ordered because the difference looked wrong.
v APPROVED: Accepted by the supervisor, so any difference is adjusted.

@ LegalCase
a forumType
s: Says what kind of body hears the case, such as a court, tribunal or arbitration.
u: Chosen when the case is opened; it decides the procedure and deadlines that apply.
c: Read together with the forum that hears it.
v COURT: A state court of law hearing the dispute.
v TRIBUNAL: A specialised tribunal, such as an employment tribunal.
v ARBITRATION: A private arbitral tribunal chosen under an arbitration agreement.
v REGULATORY_AUTHORITY: A regulator deciding on a matter.
a ourRole
s: Says which side of the dispute the business is on in this case.
u: Recorded when the case is opened.
c: Decides whether an adverse outcome means a payment or a lost recovery.
v CLAIMANT: We started the proceedings.
v DEFENDANT: Proceedings were brought against us.
v THIRD_PARTY: We are joined as a third party.
v INTERVENER: We intervene in someone else's case.
a filedDate
s: The day the proceedings were formally filed with the forum.
u: Recorded when the case starts; limitation periods and deadlines run from it, and it cannot be later than the judgment date.
a judgmentDate
s: The day judgment was given or the settlement was signed.
u: Recorded by the responsible lawyer together with the outcome; empty until the case is decided or settled.
a amountClaimed
s: The monetary amount claimed in the proceedings.
u: Taken from the claim document and used for exposure reporting; expressed in the matter's currency.
a outcome
s: A description of how the case ended, such as the judgment or settlement terms.
u: Written by the responsible lawyer with the judgment or settlement, and required before the case is closed.
a status
s: Shows where the case stands in its life, from filing through judgment to closure.
u: Moved on by the responsible lawyer.
c: Decided and settled cases must have an outcome.
v FILED: Started, with first steps under way.
v PENDING: Pleadings and hearings are in progress.
v DECIDED: Judgment has been given.
v SETTLED: Ended by a settlement between the parties.
v APPEALED: Judgment appealed to a higher court; the appeal is recorded as its own case.
v CLOSED: Finished, because the judgment became final or the appeal was decided.
v WITHDRAWN: Withdrawn or dismissed without judgment on the merits.
