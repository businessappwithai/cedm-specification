@ Campaign
a campaignType
v EMAIL: Email messages sent to a mailing list.
v EVENT: A trade show, conference or customer event.
v WEBINAR: An online seminar or live presentation, where registrations and attendance count as responses.
v ADVERTISING: Paid advertising online or in print.
v SOCIAL: Social media posts and promotions.
v DIRECT_MAIL: Letters or brochures sent by post.
v TELEMARKETING: Outbound calls to a list.
v PARTNER: Run with or by a partner.
a budgetedCost
s: The amount marketing has approved to spend on the campaign, in the campaign's currency.
u: Entered and approved when the campaign is planned, and rarely changed once it is running.
c: Compared with the actual cost to show whether the campaign is on budget.
a actualResponses
s: How many responses the campaign has produced so far, counted the way its type defines a response.
u: Updated by marketing as registrations, replies or enquiries come in, during and after the campaign.
c: Compared with the expected responses; the ones worth following up become leads.
a status
s: Where the campaign stands between planning and completion.
u: Moved on by the campaign owner as the campaign starts, finishes or is called off.
c: Only active campaigns are offered as a lead source when someone enters a new lead.
v PLANNED: Being prepared and not yet running.
v ACTIVE: Running now; leads can be attributed to it.
v COMPLETED: Finished, with its results recorded.
v CANCELLED: Called off before it finished.
@ CarePlan
a startDate
s: The day the plan of care begins and its goals and interventions take effect.
u: Set when the care team and the patient agree the plan.
c: Not later than the end date.
a nextReviewDate
s: The day the care team is due to review progress against the plan's goals.
u: Drives review reminders for the responsible clinician and the care team.
c: Moved forward at each review.
@ Carrier
a name
s: The name the carrier trades under, as dispatchers and planners know it.
u: Shown wherever a carrier is chosen for a booking or consignment.
c: The legal name is held on its party record.
a carrierType
s: What kind of transport service the carrier mainly provides, such as road, rail or air.
u: Planners filter the carriers they can book by it when choosing for a consignment.
c: A carrier offering several modes is recorded with its main one.
v ROAD: Road haulage, full or part loads.
v PARCEL: Parcel and courier networks.
v RAIL: Rail freight operators moving wagons or containers by train.
v OCEAN: Shipping lines carrying containers or bulk cargo by sea.
v AIR: Airlines and air cargo carriers.
v FORWARDER: A freight forwarder arranging transport with other carriers.
a iataCode
s: The carrier's two or three character IATA airline code, for air carriers only.
u: Printed on air waybills and used to match the carrier in airline schedules.
c: Left empty for carriers that are not airlines.
a status
s: Whether the carrier may currently be booked for shipments.
u: Moved on by logistics procurement as the carrier is onboarded, approved, suspended or retired.
c: Only approved carriers are offered when booking.
v PROSPECTIVE: Being onboarded and not yet bookable.
v APPROVED: Cleared by procurement and bookable for shipments.
v SUSPENDED: Temporarily not bookable after service or compliance problems.
v INACTIVE: No longer used and kept only for history.
@ CertificateOfAnalysis
a conclusion
s: The quality verdict on whether the material meets its specification, as stated on the certificate.
u: Entered by the quality team when results are summarised and the certificate is approved.
c: A certificate showing non-conformance is not normally issued to a customer.
v CONFORMS: Meets every specification limit that was tested.
v CONFORMS_WITH_DEVIATION: Meets the specification with an approved deviation.
v DOES_NOT_CONFORM: Fails one or more specifications.
@ Charge
a unitPrice
s: The price charged for a single unit of the billed product, service or usage.
u: Taken from the subscription plan or price list when the charge is created.
c: Stated in the charge's currency and multiplied by the quantity.
@ ChemicalBatch
a quantity
s: How much material the batch holds, as produced or received.
u: Recorded at production or goods receipt and used for yield and stock reconciliation.
c: Stated in the product's unit of measure.
@ Classification
a description
s: Explains what the classification value covers and what is left out of it.
u: Read by people classifying a record, so it should name clear examples.
c: Should say what belongs in the value and what does not.
a status
s: Whether the classification value may still be assigned to new records.
u: Set to retired by the data owner when a value is replaced or no longer wanted.
c: Records keep retired values for history.
v ACTIVE: May be assigned to records and appears in pick lists.
v RETIRED: No longer assigned, though existing records keep it.
@ Collateral
a collateralNumber
s: The unique reference by which the pledged item is known to credit officers and legal staff.
u: Assigned when the collateral is recorded during credit approval.
c: Unique across all collateral items.
a collateralType
s: What kind of security is pledged, such as property, equipment, stock or a guarantee.
u: Decides the valuation method and the haircut applied when lending value is calculated.
c: Guarantees are promises by a third party, not assets.
v REAL_ESTATE: Land or buildings, usually secured by a registered charge.
v EQUIPMENT: Machinery, vehicles or other movable assets.
v INVENTORY: Stock of goods held by the borrower, valued at cost or market and revalued often.
v RECEIVABLES: Amounts owed to the borrower by its customers.
v CASH: Cash deposits or securities.
v GUARANTEE: A guarantee given by a third party.
a valuationAmount
s: The market value of the pledged item at its most recent valuation.
u: Recorded from the valuation report and used to work out lending value after the haircut.
c: Revalued periodically.
a valuationDate
s: The day the current value of the collateral was assessed.
u: Old valuations prompt credit risk staff to order a revaluation.
c: Not in the future.
a releasedDate
s: The day the lender gave up its claim on the collateral.
u: Recorded by the credit team when the loan is repaid or the security is replaced.
c: Present only once the collateral is released.
a status
v PROPOSED: Offered during credit approval; no charge yet.
v PLEDGED: Charged in favour of the lender.
v RELEASED: Freed after repayment, so it no longer secures any loan.
v ENFORCED: Taken by the lender after default.
@ ComplianceRequirement
a obligationLevel
v MANDATORY: Must be met, so any gap has to be remedied.
v RECOMMENDED: Expected unless there is a documented reason not to.
v OPTIONAL: Good practice the business may choose to follow.
a riskRating
v LOW: Minor consequences if the requirement is missed.
v MEDIUM: Noticeable penalties or harm.
v HIGH: Heavy penalties, legal action or serious harm.
v CRITICAL: Could stop the business or cause severe harm.
@ Compound
a smiles
s: The chemical structure written as a SMILES line notation string.
u: Entered or imported at registration and used for structure searches and modelling.
c: Stored in canonical form so identical structures match.
a therapeuticArea
s: The disease area the compound is being investigated for, such as oncology or cardiology.
u: Set by the project team and used to group compounds in portfolio reviews.
c: Free text taken from the portfolio list.
@ Consignment
a transportMode
v ROAD: By truck or van.
v RAIL: By train, on a rail freight service.
v SEA: By ship, on a vessel or container service.
v AIR: By aircraft, as air cargo.
v PARCEL: By a parcel or courier network.
v MULTIMODAL: By more than one mode under one contract.
a deliveredAt
s: The date and time the goods were actually handed over at their destination.
u: Taken from the proof of delivery and used to measure on-time performance.
c: Present once the consignment is delivered.
@ ConstructionProject
a contractValue
s: The total value of the construction contracts awarded for the project.
u: Summed from the signed contracts and used by project controls and finance.
c: Compared with the project budget.
a plannedCompletionDate
s: The day the finished works are due to be handed over to the client.
u: Agreed in the main contract; delays are measured against it.
c: Moves only through an approved change.
@ Contact
a contactRole
v DECISION_MAKER: Decides whether to buy or sign.
v BUYER: Places orders on behalf of the organisation.
v TECHNICAL: Advises on technical matters.
v ACCOUNTS_PAYABLE: Receives invoices and arranges payment.
v SERVICE: Is the contact for service and support.
v OTHER: Any role not listed, described elsewhere on the contact.
a preferredChannel
s: The way the person prefers to be reached by the business, such as email or telephone.
u: Followed by sales, service and marketing staff when they reach out.
c: The contact details themselves are on the person's contact points.
v EMAIL: By email to the person's address.
v PHONE: By telephone call or voicemail.
v MESSAGING: By instant message on a chat app.
v POST: By letter sent to a postal address.
a consentDate
s: The day the person gave or withdrew consent to be contacted for marketing.
u: Recorded with every change of consent so the latest decision is dated.
c: Serves as evidence for data-protection rules.
a status
v ACTIVE: A current contact who can be approached.
v INACTIVE: No longer a contact.
@ ContactPoint
a channel
v EMAIL: An email address used for written messages.
v PHONE: A landline telephone number.
v MOBILE: A mobile telephone number.
v FAX: A fax number, kept for partners who still use it.
v WEBSITE: A web address of the party's site or page.
v MESSAGING: A handle on a messaging app.
a purpose
v GENERAL: Any purpose, when nothing more specific applies.
v BILLING: Invoices and statements sent to accounts payable.
v ORDERS: Order confirmations and purchase orders.
v DELIVERY: Delivery notices and arrival updates.
v SUPPORT: Service and support enquiries.
v MARKETING: Marketing communication, where consent allows.
a status
s: Whether the channel is still usable for reaching the party.
u: Changed when the channel stops being used or a message to it bounces.
c: Inactive and invalid channels are not offered for contact.
v ACTIVE: In use and offered for contact.
v INACTIVE: No longer used and kept for history.
v INVALID: Known not to work, such as a bouncing address.
@ ContractAmendment
a signedDate
s: The day the last party signed the amendment, completing its execution.
u: Recorded when the signed copy comes back, before the effective date arrives.
c: Present once signed.
a status
s: Where the amendment stands between drafting and taking effect.
u: Moved on by internal approval, the other party's reply and signature.
c: Only signed amendments change the contract.
v DRAFT: Being written and not yet approved or shared.
v APPROVED: Approved internally, sent to the other party.
v SIGNED: Signed by both parties.
v EFFECTIVE: Its effective date has come; the contract now reflects it.
v REJECTED: Refused by the other party.
v CANCELLED: Dropped before signature, so the contract is unchanged.
r document
s: The signed copy of the amendment held as a document.
u: Linked by the contract manager once both parties have signed.
n: At most one signed document per amendment.
w: Kept with the contract's documents as the evidence that the change was agreed.
@ ContractClause
a category
v LIABILITY: Limits or exclusions of liability.
v INDEMNITY: Indemnities between the parties.
v TERMINATION: Rights and notice to end the contract.
v CONFIDENTIALITY: Protection of confidential information.
v DATA_PROTECTION: Processing of personal data.
v PAYMENT: Payment terms, invoicing and late payment.
v WARRANTY: Warranties about goods or services.
v GOVERNING_LAW: The governing law and jurisdiction.
v INTELLECTUAL_PROPERTY: Ownership and licensing of rights.
v OTHER: Any term that fits none of the other categories.
@ ContractLine
a unitPrice
s: The price per unit agreed for this line of the contract.
u: Used to price orders placed under the contract, until the line's validity ends.
c: Stated in the line's currency.
@ ContractRenewal
a renewalNumber
s: The unique reference by which the renewal review is known to contract managers.
u: Assigned when the renewal review is opened ahead of the notice deadline.
c: Unique across all renewals.
a renewalType
s: How the contract continues into a new term, by itself, by negotiation or by option.
u: Taken from the contract's renewal clause when the review is opened.
c: An automatic renewal happens unless notice is given by the deadline.
v AUTOMATIC: Renews by itself unless a party gives notice.
v NEGOTIATED: Renews only if the parties agree new terms.
v OPTION: Renews if one party exercises its option to extend.
a newTermStart
s: The first day of the contract's new term once the renewal is confirmed.
u: Set by the contract manager when the renewal is confirmed.
c: The day after the current term ends, normally.
a newTermEnd
s: The last day of the contract's new term once the renewal is confirmed.
u: Set by the contract manager when the renewal is confirmed.
c: Not earlier than the new term start.
a decidedDate
s: The day the renewal was confirmed or declined.
u: Recorded with the decision so the notice deadline can be checked afterwards.
c: Present once decided.
a status
s: Whether the renewal has been decided or is still being considered.
u: Moved on by the contract manager as the decision is taken.
c: Undecided renewals near their notice deadline are escalated.
v UNDER_REVIEW: Being considered, with no decision yet.
v CONFIRMED: The contract continues for the new term.
v DECLINED: The contract ends at the current term end.
@ ContractTermination
a terminationNumber
s: The unique reference by which the termination is known to legal and contract staff.
u: Assigned when the notice is recorded against the contract.
c: Unique across all terminations.
a initiatedBy
s: Which side of the contract gave notice and brought it to an end.
u: Recorded with the notice, so exit obligations fall on the right party.
c: Read together with the ground.
v US: We gave notice and ended it.
v COUNTERPARTY: The other party ended it.
v MUTUAL: Both parties agreed to end it.
a ground
s: The reason in law or in the contract for which it was ended.
u: Chosen when the notice is recorded; it decides whether penalties or damages may apply.
c: Termination for breach usually needs evidence of the breach.
v CONVENIENCE: Ended under a right to terminate without cause.
v BREACH: Ended because the other party breached it.
v INSOLVENCY: Ended because a party became insolvent.
v EXPIRY: Ended at the end of its term without renewal.
v MUTUAL_AGREEMENT: Ended by agreement between both parties.
v FORCE_MAJEURE: Ended because performance became impossible.
a noticeDate
s: The day the termination notice was given or received.
u: The notice period runs from this day, so it sets the earliest effective date.
c: Not later than the effective date.
a reason
s: The circumstances behind the termination, written out in plain words.
u: Entered by the person recording it and read later in any dispute.
c: Free text.
a status
v NOTIFIED: Notice given or received; the contract still runs.
v EFFECTIVE: The contract has ended; exit work under way.
v COMPLETED: Exit work done, and the contract is closed.
v WITHDRAWN: Notice withdrawn by agreement; the contract continues.
@ Control
a effectiveness
v NOT_TESTED: Not yet tested, so its effectiveness is unknown.
v EFFECTIVE: Designed and operating as intended.
v PARTIALLY_EFFECTIVE: Works, but with weaknesses that need remedying.
v INEFFECTIVE: Does not achieve its purpose.
a lastTestedDate
s: The day the control was most recently tested for effectiveness.
u: Set by the tester when a test is completed; testing plans prioritise controls not tested recently.
c: Empty until the first test.
