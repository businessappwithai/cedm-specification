@ Milestone
s: A significant planned achievement or checkpoint in a project or process.
b: A project's progress is judged against its milestones. Naming each checkpoint and its target date lets sponsors see whether the work is on track without reading every task.
u: Set by the project manager at planning; updated as the date approaches; read by sponsors and steering groups.
c: A milestone belongs to one Project and is reached by completing a set of ProjectTasks.
l: A milestone is planned, may be flagged at risk and recover, and ends achieved, missed or cancelled.
x: "Go-live" for a system implementation, targeted for 1 September and flagged at risk in July.
a milestoneId
s: The identity of the milestone.
u: Generated on creation; tasks refer to it.
a code
s: The reference of the milestone within the project, such as M3.
u: Assigned by the project manager.
a name
s: The name of the checkpoint.
u: Shown on plans and status reports.
a targetDate
s: The date by which the milestone should be reached.
u: Set at planning; reviewed at status meetings.
a status
s: How the milestone is progressing.
u: Updated by the project manager at status reviews.
v PLANNED: Not yet reached and expected on time.
v AT_RISK: Likely to be late without action.
v ACHIEVED: Reached. A final state.
v MISSED: Passed without being reached. A final state.
v CANCELLED: No longer needed. A final state.
r project
s: The project the milestone belongs to.
u: Set at planning.
n: Exactly one project: a milestone is a checkpoint of a single undertaking.
w: Rolls the milestone's status up to the project.
r tasks
s: The tasks whose completion leads to the milestone.
u: Linked at planning.
n: Any number of tasks.
w: Task progress feeds the milestone's status.

@ MiningSite
s: A geographic site where mineral or natural-resource extraction takes place.
b: The site is the unit of mining operations: it has a resource, a stage from exploration to closure, and a production history. Safety, permits, output and costs are all reported by site.
u: Registered when exploration begins; advanced as it is developed and brought into production; read by operations and compliance.
c: A site stands at a Location, is operated by an Organization and has ProductionRecords.
l: A site begins in exploration, moves to development and then to active production. It can be suspended and resumed, and is closed at the end of its life or if abandoned.
x: The Kiruna iron ore mine, in production since 1900 and operated by the mining company.
a miningSiteId
s: The identity of the site.
u: Generated on registration.
a siteCode
s: The code of the site, such as KIR-01.
u: Unique; used in production reports.
a name
s: The name of the site.
u: Shown in reports.
a resourceType
s: The mineral or resource extracted.
u: Entered at registration; used in production and market reports.
a status
s: The stage of the site's life.
u: Set by operations management.
v EXPLORATION: Being surveyed to prove a resource.
v DEVELOPMENT: Being prepared for production.
v ACTIVE: In production.
v SUSPENDED: Production paused.
v CLOSED: Permanently closed. A final state.
r location
s: Where the site is.
u: Set at registration.
n: Exactly one location.
w: Provides coordinates and jurisdiction.
r organization
s: The organisation that operates the site.
u: Set at registration.
n: Exactly one operator, responsible for permits and safety.
w: Determines who answers for operations.
r productionRecords
s: The production records of the site.
u: Read to measure output.
n: Any number, one for each production period.
w: Record what the site has produced.

@ Nonconformance
a nonconformanceId
s: The identity of the nonconformance.
u: Generated on recording; corrective actions and verifications refer to it.
a severity
s: How serious the deviation is.
u: Set by quality at review; drives containment and escalation.
v LOW: Minor deviation with little effect.
v MEDIUM: Notable deviation needing correction.
v HIGH: Serious deviation affecting product or customers.
v CRITICAL: Dangerous or legally significant deviation needing immediate containment.
a status
s: Where the nonconformance is in its handling.
u: Moved by quality staff.
v OPEN: Recorded but not yet reviewed.
v UNDER_REVIEW: Being investigated.
v CONTAINED: Affected goods are isolated so the problem cannot spread.
v CORRECTIVE_ACTION: Corrective actions are under way to remove the cause.
v CLOSED: Resolved and verified. A final state.
v REJECTED: Reviewed and found not to be a nonconformance. A final state.

@ NonprofitCampaign
s: A fundraising or public-interest campaign run to reach a defined funding or participation objective.
b: Nonprofits raise money and support in campaigns. Recording the objective and target lets the organisation see what a campaign raised, from whom, and whether it succeeded.
u: Planned by the fundraising team; launched; tracked; completed; read by management and donors.
c: A campaign is run by an Organization, draws on donor Parties and is supported by Documents.
l: A campaign is planned, active while running, and ends completed or cancelled.
x: The winter appeal to raise 250,000 for the emergency shelter programme.
a campaignId
s: The identity of the campaign.
u: Generated on creation; donations refer to it.
a campaignCode
s: The code of the campaign, such as WINTER26.
u: Unique; used in appeal tracking.
a name
s: The name of the campaign.
u: Shown in reports and donor communications.
a objective
s: What the campaign aims to achieve.
u: Written at planning; read when judging results.
a targetAmount
s: The amount the campaign aims to raise.
u: Set at planning; not negative; progress is measured against it.
a status
s: Where the campaign is.
u: Updated by the fundraising team.
v PLANNED: Being prepared.
v ACTIVE: Running.
v COMPLETED: Finished. A final state.
v CANCELLED: Called off. A final state.
r organization
s: The organisation running the campaign.
u: Set at planning.
n: Exactly one organisation: a campaign is run in the name of a single body.
w: Determines whose cause and accounts the campaign serves.
r donors
s: The people and organisations who gave to the campaign.
u: Added as gifts are received.
n: Any number of donors.
w: Build the campaign's donor list.
r documents
s: The documents of the campaign, such as appeal letters and reports.
u: Attached during the campaign.
n: Any number of documents.
w: Form the campaign's record.
