@ Failure
s: An observed failure of equipment or an asset, recording the symptom, when it occurred, its effect and the maintenance context.
b: Reliability engineering starts with knowing what failed, when and how badly. A failure record keeps that observation separate from the repair work, so the history of what went wrong is not lost when it is fixed.
u: Raised by operators or technicians when something breaks; linked to the repair work order; analysed for causes and trends.
c: A failure concerns one piece of Equipment; repairs and cause analysis refer back to it.
l: Finalised evidence is corrected by compensating or superseding records and never overwritten.
x: A conveyor drive motor tripped on overload at 03:10 and stopped the line for forty minutes.
a failureId
s: The identity of the failure record.
u: Generated when raised; repair orders and analyses refer to it.
a effectiveAt
s: When the failure occurred.
u: Entered by the person reporting it; used to measure time between failures and downtime.
r equipment
s: The equipment that failed.
u: Chosen when the failure is raised.
n: Exactly one equipment item: a failure is always a failure of something specific.
w: Builds the failure history of each machine.

@ Farm
s: A managed agricultural operation consisting of land, buildings, resources and production activities.
b: A farm is the unit that plans what to grow or raise and answers for what it produces. Fields, crops and equipment are organised under it.
u: Registered when an operation is established; read by agronomy, operations and sales.
c: A farm stands at a Location, is owned by Parties, is divided into FarmFields and grows Crops.
l: A farm is planned, goes active when production starts, may be inactive in a fallow period, and is retired when the operation ends.
x: Greenfield Farm, a mixed crop and livestock operation of 340 hectares.
a farmId
s: The identity of the farm record.
u: Generated on registration.
a farmCode
s: The code by which the farm is known in plans and reports.
u: Unique; assigned at registration.
a name
s: The name of the farm.
u: Shown in lists and on documents.
a farmType
s: The main kind of production the farm carries out.
u: Chosen at registration; groups farms in reports.
v CROP: Grows crops.
v LIVESTOCK: Raises animals.
v MIXED: Both crops and livestock.
v AQUACULTURE: Farms fish or other aquatic species.
v OTHER: Any other kind.
a status
s: Whether the farm is producing.
u: Set by the operator.
v PLANNED: Established on paper but not producing.
v ACTIVE: In production.
v INACTIVE: Temporarily not producing.
v RETIRED: Operation ended. A final state.
r location
s: The land and site of the farm.
u: Set at registration.
n: Exactly one location: a farm is a place.
w: Supplies address and boundaries.
r owner
s: The parties that own the farm.
u: Recorded at registration.
n: Any number of owners, including joint owners.
w: Identifies who is entitled to the farm's output.
r fields
s: The fields that make up the farm.
u: Added as land is surveyed.
n: Any number of fields.
w: Fields are managed as part of the farm.
r crops
s: The crops grown on the farm.
u: Linked from planting plans.
n: Any number of crops.
w: Shows the farm's production mix.

@ FarmField
s: A defined area of agricultural land that is managed as a unit of production.
b: Crops are planted, treated and harvested field by field. A field record lets the farm plan rotations and track yields for each area.
u: Defined when land is surveyed; planted with crops; read for plans and harvest records.
c: A field belongs to a Farm and is planted with Crops.
l: A field is active while cropped, may be left fallow to recover, and becomes inactive when taken out of use.
x: Field N-12, 18 hectares of clay loam, planted with winter wheat.
a farmFieldId
s: The identity of the field.
u: Generated on creation.
a fieldCode
s: The code of the field within the farm, such as N-12.
u: Assigned by the farm; used in work plans.
a area
s: The size of the field.
u: Measured or surveyed; read with the unit.
a areaUnit
s: The unit in which the area is expressed.
u: Chosen with the area.
a soilType
s: The main soil of the field.
u: Entered from soil surveys; used in crop choice.
a status
s: What the field is doing.
u: Set by the farm.
v ACTIVE: In cultivation.
v FALLOW: Resting without a crop to restore the soil.
v INACTIVE: Out of use.
r farm
s: The farm the field belongs to.
u: Set at creation.
n: Exactly one farm: land is managed within a single holding.
w: Rolls the field's results up to the farm.
r crops
s: The crops grown in the field.
u: Linked as plantings are made.
n: Any number of crops over time.
w: Records the rotation history.

@ Fee
a feeId
s: The identity of the fee.
u: Generated on creation; invoices and payments refer to it.
a feeCode
s: The code of the fee type, such as TUITION-2026 or LAB.
u: Chosen when the fee is assessed.
a amount
s: The amount charged.
u: Calculated from the fee schedule.
a dueDate
s: The date payment is due.
u: Set when assessed.
a status
v DRAFT: Being prepared; not yet charged.
v ASSESSED: Charged to the student but not yet invoiced.
v INVOICED: Included on an invoice.
v PAID: Settled.
v WAIVED: Excused by the institution.
v CANCELLED: Withdrawn.
r student
n: Exactly one student: a fee is always charged to a specific learner.
r currency
n: Exactly one currency, so the amount is unambiguous.

@ Filing
s: A submission made to a court, regulator, registry or authority, with immutable evidence of what was submitted.
b: Legal and regulatory duties are met by filing. The record shows what was filed, with whom and when, which is the proof that a duty was met on time.
u: Created when a document is submitted; read when proving compliance or responding to the authority.
c: A filing is directed to an external body and supported by documents.
l: Finalised evidence is preserved; corrections or supersession are explicit filings.
x: Annual accounts filed with the company registry on 28 June.
a filingId
s: The identity of the filing.
u: Generated on creation.
a occurredAt
s: When the filing was submitted.
u: Taken from the receipt; deadlines are checked against it.

@ Finding
s: An observation of a deficiency in a control, audit, compliance, quality or risk area that needs a decision or remediation.
b: Audits are only useful if what they find is followed up. A finding states the problem, how serious it is and what has to be done, so it can be tracked to closure.
u: Raised by auditors or reviewers; assigned for remediation; reviewed until closed.
c: A finding may concern a Control or a Risk.
l: Finalised evidence is not silently rewritten; corrections or supersession are explicit.
x: Finding that bank reconciliations were reviewed late in three of twelve months.
a findingId
s: The identity of the finding.
u: Generated when raised.
a effectiveAt
s: When the finding was raised.
u: Set at issue.
r control
s: The control the finding is about.
u: Set where a specific control failed.
n: At most one control.
w: Links the deficiency to the control to be fixed.
r risk
n: At most one risk, the exposure that the finding relates to.
