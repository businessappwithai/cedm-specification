@ Product
s: A canonical business offering or managed item that can be bought, sold, stocked, consumed, delivered or subscribed to, and that other processes refer to.
b: Product is the shared definition of what the business deals in. Sales, purchasing, stock, manufacturing and finance all refer to the same product record, so a price, a stock level and an invoice line are about the same thing.
u: Created by product management when an item enters the catalogue; maintained over its life; referenced on almost every order, movement and invoice line.
c: A product belongs to a ProductCategory and Brand, is supplied by Suppliers, is stored at Locations, has variants, attributes, packaging and a lifecycle history, and appears on order, stock and invoice lines.
l: A product is drafted, becomes active when approved for trade, can be blocked while an issue is resolved, and ends discontinued or retired. History and past documents keep their reference.
x: Item A-200, "Cordless drill 18V", a finished good in the Power Tools category, sold in cartons of four.
a productId
s: The identity of the product, which never changes even when its code or name does.
u: Generated on creation; order lines, stock and invoices refer to it.
a code
s: The code by which staff and systems name the product.
u: Unique; assigned by product management and used in orders, price lists and integrations.
a name
s: The name of the product as shown to users and customers.
u: Shown in catalogues, on documents and in search results.
a description
s: A longer description of what the product is.
u: Written by product management; used on websites, quotes and datasheets.
a productType
s: What kind of offering the product is, which decides how it is bought, stocked and delivered.
u: Chosen on creation; stock rules apply only to physical types.
v GOOD: A finished physical item that is bought or sold and held in stock.
v MATERIAL: A raw material or component consumed in production.
v SERVICE: Work performed for a customer, not held in stock.
v SUBSCRIPTION: A recurring entitlement billed on a schedule.
v ASSET: A durable item the business keeps and depreciates.
v BUNDLE: A package of other products sold together.
v OTHER: Anything else that is traded.
a status
s: Whether the product can currently be traded.
u: Set by product management; new orders are accepted only for ACTIVE products.
v DRAFT: Being set up; not yet tradable.
v ACTIVE: Available for ordinary trading.
v DISCONTINUED: Being phased out; existing stock may be sold but it is not reordered. A final state.
v BLOCKED: Temporarily barred from trading, for example during a quality or compliance issue.
v RETIRED: Removed from the catalogue; kept for history. A final state.
a sku
s: The stock-keeping unit code used in warehouses and on retail systems.
u: Assigned at creation; printed on labels and scanned during handling.
a unitOfMeasure
s: The base unit in which the product is counted, such as each or kilogram.
u: Chosen on creation; stock and order quantities are converted to it.
a standardPrice
s: The standard selling price of the product.
u: Set by pricing; quotes and orders start from it before discounts.
a taxCategory
s: The tax category that decides how the product is taxed.
u: Chosen on creation; tax rules look it up when documents are priced.
a createdAt
s: When the product record was created.
u: Set by the system.
a updatedAt
s: When the product record was last changed.
u: Set by the system.
r category
s: The category the product is classified in.
u: Chosen on creation; used for assortment and reporting.
n: At most one category; uncategorised products are allowed while being set up.
w: Places the product in the catalogue hierarchy.
r suppliers
s: The suppliers from which the product can be bought.
u: Linked as sources are approved.
n: Any number of suppliers.
w: Defines the sourcing options for purchasing.
r locations
s: The locations where the product is held or sold.
u: Linked as the product is stocked.
n: Any number of locations.
w: Defines where stock may exist.
r salesOrderLines
s: The sales order lines that sell the product.
u: Read for sales history.
n: Any number, growing as the product sells.
w: Show demand for the product.
r purchaseOrderLines
s: The purchase order lines that buy the product.
u: Read for purchasing history.
n: Any number.
w: Show supply commitments.
r inventoryBalances
s: The stock balances held of the product.
u: Read for stock levels.
n: Any number, one for each location holding it.
w: Show how much is available where.
r inventoryMovements
s: The stock movements of the product.
u: Read for stock history.
n: Any number over the product's life.
w: Explain how stock reached its level.
r invoiceLines
s: The invoice lines that bill the product.
u: Read for revenue and cost analysis.
n: Any number.
w: Connect the product to money.
r lots
n: Any number of lots, for lot-controlled products.
r serialNumbers
n: Any number of serial numbers, for serialised products.
r brand
n: At most one brand under which the product is marketed.
r variants
n: Any number of variants, such as sizes and colours.
r attributes
n: Any number of descriptive attributes.
r packagingConfigurations
n: Any number of packaging configurations.
r lifecycleHistory
n: Any number of lifecycle records, one for each status change.
r inventoryItems
n: Any number of inventory profiles, one for each organisation that stocks the product.
r serviceOrders
n: Any number of service orders that concern the product.
r entitlements
n: Any number of entitlements to service for the product.
