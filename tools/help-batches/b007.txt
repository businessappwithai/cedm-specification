@ BusinessTransaction
s: A recorded business transaction that commits, changes or represents a business state or obligation, in a form common to every domain.
b: Many different activities, such as sales, purchases, transfers and adjustments, share the same bones: a number, a date, an amount, the parties and products involved, and a status. This record carries those common facts so they can be reported, audited and reversed uniformly.
u: Created when a business transaction is entered or generated by a process; moved through approval and posting; read for history, exposure and reporting.
c: A transaction belongs to an Organization, involves Parties and Products, may be evidenced by a Document and may take place at a Location; its amounts are in one Currency.
l: A transaction is drafted, opened, approved and posted, and finally completed. It can be cancelled before it is posted. Once posted it is never edited: an error is corrected by reversing it and entering a new one.
x: Transaction SO-20413, a sale of 40 units to a retailer, approved on Monday, posted to the ledger on Tuesday and completed when delivery was confirmed.
a transactionId
s: The identity of the transaction.
u: Generated on creation; documents, postings and reversals refer to the transaction through it.
a transactionNumber
s: The number under which the transaction is known to users and partners.
u: Allocated from a number series for its type; unique within the owning context and transaction type, and quoted on documents.
a transactionType
s: The kind of transaction, such as SALE, PURCHASE, TRANSFER or ADJUSTMENT.
u: Chosen when the transaction is created; decides numbering, approval rules and how posting treats the amount.
a transactionDate
s: The business date and time of the transaction.
u: Entered or defaulted to now; used for period assignment, reporting and the order of events.
a status
s: How far the transaction has progressed from draft to completion.
u: Moved by the business process as steps are performed; posted transactions are no longer changed.
v DRAFT: Being entered; nothing is committed.
v OPEN: Submitted and awaiting approval.
v APPROVED: Authorised but not yet posted.
v POSTED: Recorded in the books; it can now only be reversed, not edited.
v COMPLETED: Fully performed and closed out.
v CANCELLED: Withdrawn before being posted; no effect on the books.
v REVERSED: Posted and then undone by a reversing transaction; the original stays on record.
a currencyId
s: The currency in which the transaction's amounts are expressed.
u: Set when the transaction is created; the total and any line amounts are in this currency.
a totalAmount
s: The total value of the transaction in its currency.
u: Calculated from its lines or entered; non-negative unless the transaction type explicitly allows signed amounts.
a referenceNumber
s: An external reference for the transaction, such as a customer's purchase order number.
u: Entered to link the transaction to a partner's document; used for search and reconciliation.
a createdAt
s: When the transaction record was created.
u: Set by the system and never edited; part of the audit trail.
a updatedAt
s: When the transaction record was last changed.
u: Set by the system on every change; used to detect recent activity and in synchronisation.
r organization
s: The organisation on whose behalf the transaction is made.
u: Set when the transaction is created; scopes numbering, approvals and ledger postings.
n: At most one organisation; when none is given the transaction belongs to the default business context.
w: Determines whose books and permissions apply.
r parties
s: The parties involved, such as customer, supplier or counterparty.
u: Added as the transaction is entered; read to show who the transaction concerns.
n: Any number, including none for an internal transaction.
w: Gives each party a view of the transactions they take part in.
r products
s: The products, goods or services the transaction concerns.
u: Added from the catalogue as the transaction is entered.
n: Any number; some transactions, such as payments, involve no products.
w: Connects the transaction to what was bought, sold or moved.
r document
s: The document that evidences the transaction, such as an order form or invoice.
u: Attached when the paper or electronic evidence exists.
n: At most one primary document.
w: Gives auditors the source evidence.
r location
s: The place at which the transaction occurred or applies.
u: Set when location matters for tax, stock or reporting.
n: At most one location.
w: Drives location-specific rules such as tax and inventory.

@ BusinessUnit
s: A major business, division, product line or operating segment within an organisation.
b: Business units are how a large organisation divides its activity for management and reporting. They let results, budgets and responsibility be assigned to a meaningful segment rather than to the organisation as a whole.
u: Defined by management and finance; assigned to people, transactions and budgets; read when reporting by segment.
c: Each business unit belongs to one Organization and groups the work and results of one part of it.
l: A business unit keeps its code and identity stable. Reorganisations are recorded explicitly so earlier reports are not silently changed.
x: The Consumer Electronics division of a manufacturer, with its own budget and profit target.
a businessUnitId
s: The identity of the business unit.
u: Generated on creation; budgets, transactions and reports refer to it.
a code
s: The short reference for the unit, such as CE or EMEA-RETAIL.
u: Assigned by finance and used on reports and in postings; kept stable because history relies on it.
a name
s: The full name of the unit.
u: Shown in organisation charts and management reports.
r organization
s: The organisation to which the unit belongs.
u: Set when the unit is created.
n: Exactly one organisation; a unit cannot stand alone.
w: Rolls the unit's results up into its parent organisation.

@ Calendar
s: A calendar that defines business dates, working days, holidays and time-control rules used for planning and operations.
b: Whether a date counts as a working day is not obvious: it depends on region, industry and company. A calendar states that explicitly, so due dates, delivery promises and schedules all agree.
u: Maintained by administrators; referenced by schedulers, service-level calculations and planning when they need to know which days count.
c: Locations, teams, contracts and schedules point to the calendar that governs their working days.
l: A calendar's code and identity stay stable. Changes to holidays apply from their effective date and do not rewrite history.
x: The German national business calendar, with weekends and public holidays marked non-working.
a calendarId
s: The identity of the calendar.
u: Generated on creation; schedules and rules refer to the calendar through it.
a code
s: The short reference for the calendar, such as DE-NAT.
u: Assigned by the administrator; used in configuration and reports; kept stable.
a name
s: The descriptive name of the calendar.
u: Shown wherever a calendar is chosen.

@ Campaign
s: A marketing or outreach initiative aimed at an audience over a defined period, with its responses and outcomes measured.
b: Campaigns are how an organisation deliberately reaches out to win or retain customers. Recording them lets the business see what was done, who was targeted and what it achieved.
u: Planned and launched by marketing; linked to audiences, activities and opportunities; reviewed when results are analysed.
c: A campaign connects to the parties it targets, the activities carried out and the opportunities it generates.
l: Campaigns are planned, run and closed. History is preserved so results can be compared and nothing is counted twice downstream.
x: A spring promotion email series to lapsed customers, which produced 120 reactivations.
a campaignId
s: The identity of the campaign.
u: Generated on creation; responses and results refer to the campaign through it.
a code
s: A short reference for the campaign, such as SPRING26-WINBACK.
u: Assigned by marketing and used in tracking links, reports and attribution.

@ CarePlan
s: A plan of care that sets out a patient's goals, the interventions to reach them, who is responsible and when to follow up.
b: A care plan turns a diagnosis into coordinated action. It lets everyone involved in a patient's care see the same goals and responsibilities, and lets progress be checked against them.
u: Drawn up by the care team after assessment; reviewed at follow-up; consulted by everyone involved in the patient's care.
c: Each plan belongs to one HealthcarePatient and ties together the interventions and follow-up for that person.
l: A plan is drafted, becomes active when agreed, and completes when goals are met or care ends, or is cancelled if never put into effect. Completed plans are kept and amended through explicit follow-up, not rewritten.
x: A post-surgical recovery plan with mobility goals, physiotherapy sessions and a two-week review.
a carePlanId
s: The identity of the care plan.
u: Generated on creation; tasks and reviews refer to the plan through it.
a carePlanCode
s: The reference by which the plan is cited in the record and in handovers.
u: Assigned when the plan is created; unique.
a status
s: Whether the plan is being drafted, in force, or finished.
u: Moved by the care team.
v DRAFT: Being prepared and not yet agreed with the patient or team.
v ACTIVE: Agreed and in force; the team is delivering it.
v COMPLETED: Goals met or care ended; the plan is closed but kept on record.
v CANCELLED: Withdrawn before it took effect.
r context
s: The patient the plan is for.
u: Set when the plan is created and not changed.
n: Exactly one patient; a patient may have several plans over time.
w: Attaches the plan to the patient's record so every clinician sees it.

@ Carrier
s: A logistics provider that transports shipments, consignments or loads.
b: Goods usually move on someone else's trucks, ships or planes. A carrier record identifies that provider and gives bookings, tracking and freight invoices a single party to refer to.
u: Created when a transport provider is approved for use; selected when shipments are booked; read for service performance and freight costs.
c: A carrier is a Party acting in the transport role; shipments and consignments are assigned to it.
l: Changes and retries preserve history, and a carrier's past shipments remain linked to it after it stops being used.
x: A regional haulage firm engaged for pallet deliveries within the country.
a carrierId
s: The identity of the carrier role.
u: Generated on creation; shipments and rates refer to the carrier through it.
a code
s: The carrier's short code, such as the SCAC code used in North American freight.
u: Entered when the carrier is set up; used on shipping documents and in EDI messages.
r party
n: Exactly one party: the carrier role is played by a single legal or natural person.

@ CashPosition
a cashPositionId
s: The identity of this cash snapshot.
a asOf
s: The cut-off date and time for which the position is calculated.
a ledgerBalance
s: The balance according to the books at the cut-off, including items not yet cleared at the bank.
a availableBalance
s: The amount that can actually be spent at the cut-off, after holds and pending items.
a forecastBalance
s: The expected balance at a future date, based on scheduled receipts and payments.
r bankAccount
n: Exactly one bank account; each snapshot describes a single account in a single currency.
r bankTransactions
u: Read to explain how the position was derived and to trace each component back to the statement.
r payments
n: Any number of payments; those pending or scheduled at the cut-off feed the available and forecast figures.

@ CertificateOfAnalysis
a certificateOfAnalysisId
s: The identity of the certificate.
a certificateNumber
s: The unique number printed on the certificate.
a issuedAt
s: When the certificate was issued to its recipient.
a status
v DRAFT: Being prepared from the inspection results; not yet approved.
v APPROVED: Approved by quality but not yet released to the customer.
v ISSUED: Released to the recipient; it now stands as evidence.
v SUPERSEDED: Replaced by a corrected certificate; kept for history.
v VOID: Withdrawn because it was wrong or issued in error; kept for history.
r goodsReceipt
n: At most one receipt; set when the certificate documents incoming material.
r shipment
n: At most one shipment; set when the certificate accompanies outgoing goods.
