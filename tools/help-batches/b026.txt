@ Job
s: A reusable definition of work responsibilities, qualifications and classification that positions are created from and people are employed into.
b: A job is the description of a kind of work, such as Accountant or Warehouse Operator, separate from any particular vacancy. Defining it once keeps titles, grades and requirements consistent across the many positions that share it.
u: Defined by HR; used when positions are created and filled; read by recruiters, managers and compensation analysts.
c: Positions are instances of a job.
l: A job is drafted, becomes active when approved for use, may be inactive while not needed, and is retired when withdrawn. Existing positions and history are kept.
x: The job "Senior Accountant", with responsibilities, required qualifications and pay grade, used by five positions across three departments.
a jobId
s: The identity of the job definition.
u: Generated on creation; positions refer to it.
a code
s: The job code in the organisation's job catalogue, such as ACC-SR.
u: Unique; assigned by HR and used in reports and pay structures.
a title
s: The standard title of the job.
u: Shown on job advertisements, contracts and organisation charts.
a status
s: Whether the job definition is available for new positions.
u: Set by HR; only ACTIVE jobs can be used to create positions.
v DRAFT: Being written; not yet approved.
v ACTIVE: Approved and available for positions.
v INACTIVE: Temporarily not offered; existing positions continue.
v RETIRED: Withdrawn; kept for history. A final state.
r positions
s: The positions that are instances of this job.
u: Read to see where in the organisation the job is used.
n: Any number of positions, from none for a new job to many for a common one.
w: Changes to the job description flow to every position of the job.

@ JournalEntry
a journalEntryId
s: The identity of the accounting entry.
u: Generated on creation; ledger lines and audit refer to it.
a entryNumber
s: The sequential number of the entry in the ledger.
u: Allocated from the ledger's number series; unique; quoted in audit queries.
a entryDate
s: The accounting date of the entry.
u: Set from the originating event; decides the fiscal period the entry belongs to.
a status
s: Whether the entry has been recorded in the books.
u: Moved by the posting process; only POSTED entries affect balances.
v DRAFT: Prepared but not yet recorded; no effect on balances.
v POSTED: Recorded in the ledger; it affects balances and can no longer be edited.
v REVERSED: Cancelled by a reversing entry; the original stays on record. A final state.
a description
s: A short explanation of what the entry records.
u: Written or generated at posting; shown in ledgers and drill-downs.
r lines
n: At least two lines, because an entry has a debit and a credit side which must balance.
r sourceTransaction
n: At most one source transaction; entries made by hand have none.
r payment
n: At most one payment, the payment that gave rise to the entry.
r creditNote
n: At most one credit note, the document that gave rise to the entry.
r organization
n: At most one organisation, the ledger owner; it defaults to the current ledger.

@ JournalEntryLine
s: One debit or credit line within a journal entry.
b: Double-entry accounting records every event as at least two lines which balance. The line is the unit of the ledger: an account, an amount and a direction.
u: Created with its entry; summed per account to give balances; read by financial reports and audits.
c: Each line belongs to one JournalEntry and posts to one Account.
l: Lines are part of their entry: they are edited while it is a draft and fixed once it is posted.
x: Debit 1,200.00 to Accounts Receivable as part of the entry for invoice INV-2026-0441.
a journalEntryLineId
s: The identity of the ledger line.
u: Generated on creation; drill-downs and reconciliations refer to it.
a lineNumber
s: The position of the line within the entry.
u: Set when the entry is created; keeps the order of lines stable.
a debitAmount
s: The amount debited to the account.
u: Entered or derived from the source; not negative, and zero if the line is a credit.
a creditAmount
s: The amount credited to the account.
u: Entered or derived from the source; not negative, and zero if the line is a debit.
a description
s: A note about this line.
u: Shown in ledger drill-downs.
r journalEntry
s: The entry the line belongs to.
u: Set when the line is created.
n: Exactly one entry: a line has no meaning outside the entry it balances.
w: The entry's lines must balance before it can be posted.
r account
s: The ledger account the line posts to.
u: Chosen when the entry is made.
n: Exactly one account: each line affects a single account.
w: Determines which balance and statement line the amount affects.

@ Language
s: A language used for localisation, communication preferences, content and reporting.
b: People read and write in different languages. A language record lets the application offer translations, remember preferences and tag content with the language it is written in.
u: Loaded from the standard language registry; chosen on user profiles, documents and messages.
c: Parties, users and content refer to a language; the language itself depends on nothing.
l: Code and identity stay stable, and history is never silently rewritten.
x: English, with code en.
a languageId
s: The identity of the language record.
u: Generated when the language is loaded.
a code
s: The two-letter ISO 639-1 code of the language, such as en or de.
u: Taken from the standard; unique; used in locale settings and APIs.
a name
s: The name of the language in English.
u: Shown in language pick-lists.

@ Lead
s: A prospective customer or business opportunity before it has been qualified.
b: Sales teams cannot chase every enquiry equally. A lead records a possible customer and where it came from, so the team can decide which are worth pursuing and measure how well each source performs.
u: Created from forms, events, referrals or imports; qualified by sales; converted into a customer and opportunity or discarded; read by sales managers.
c: A lead may identify a Party or Customer, is owned by a Party (the salesperson), and may become an Opportunity.
l: A lead is new, goes through qualification, and ends qualified and converted into an opportunity, or disqualified or lost.
x: A visitor who downloads a whitepaper is recorded as a lead from the website source, qualified by a call and converted to an opportunity.
a leadId
s: The identity of the lead.
u: Generated on creation; activities and conversion refer to it.
a leadNumber
s: The lead's reference number.
u: Allocated on creation; unique; quoted by sales staff.
a status
s: How far the lead has been worked.
u: Moved by sales.
v NEW: Captured but not yet worked.
v QUALIFYING: Being assessed for fit and interest.
v QUALIFIED: Confirmed as a genuine prospect.
v DISQUALIFIED: Judged not to be a fit. A final state.
v CONVERTED: Turned into a customer and opportunity. A final state.
v LOST: The prospect went elsewhere or stopped responding. A final state.
a source
s: Where the lead came from, such as web, event or referral.
u: Chosen at capture; used to measure which channels produce customers.
a estimatedValue
s: The potential value of the business.
u: Estimated by sales; used to prioritise.
a createdAt
s: When the lead was captured.
u: Set by the system; used to measure response time.
r party
s: The person or organisation the lead is about.
u: Linked once the prospect is identified.
n: At most one party; a lead may be captured before the prospect is identified.
w: Keeps contact details in one place.
r customer
s: The customer record, once the lead has become one.
u: Set on conversion.
n: At most one customer.
w: Records the outcome of conversion.
r owner
s: The salesperson responsible for the lead.
u: Assigned by sales management.
n: At most one owner; unassigned leads wait in a queue.
w: Gives accountability for follow-up.
r opportunity
s: The opportunity created from the lead.
u: Set on conversion.
n: At most one opportunity.
w: Links pipeline to the leads that created it.
