@ Lead
a leadNumber
s: The reference number that identifies this prospective customer or opportunity before it has been qualified.
u: Allocated by the system when the lead is created; unique, never reused, and quoted by sales staff in follow-up and reports.
a status
s: How far the sales team has worked the lead, from first contact to conversion or loss.
u: Moved by sales as it is qualified; a qualified lead can be converted into an opportunity, while disqualified, converted and lost leads are not worked again.
a estimatedValue
s: The potential revenue sales expects if the lead becomes a customer, in the lead's currency.
u: Estimated by the salesperson and revised as the lead is qualified; sales managers use it to prioritise effort and forecast the pipeline.
@ Lease
a leaseType
s: The kind of lease, which decides its accounting and legal treatment.
u: Chosen when the lease is created and not expected to change; finance uses it to decide how payments and the asset are recorded.
v OPERATING: A lease where the lessor keeps the risks and rewards of ownership.
v FINANCE: A lease that transfers substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to the lessee.
v COMMERCIAL: A lease of business premises such as offices, shops or industrial units.
v RESIDENTIAL: A lease of a home to a tenant for living in.
v EQUIPMENT: A lease of machinery, vehicles or other movable equipment.
v OTHER: Any lease that fits none of the other types, described in its terms.
a status
s: Where the lease stands, from negotiation through occupation to its end.
u: Moved by the lessor or property manager; rent is charged only while the lease is ACTIVE, and ended leases stay on record.
v DRAFT: Being negotiated or prepared and not yet binding.
v ACTIVE: In force, with the lessee entitled to use the property or asset and rent falling due.
v SUSPENDED: Paused, for example during a dispute or an agreed rent holiday.
v EXPIRED: Ran to its agreed end date. A final state.
v TERMINATED: Ended early by agreement or breach. A final state.
v CANCELLED: Withdrawn before it ever started. A final state.
@ LeaveRequest
a leaveType
s: The category of absence the employee is asking for, which decides how it is paid and counted.
u: Chosen by the employee when requesting; leave policy, payroll and entitlement checks use it to decide which balance is reduced.
v ANNUAL: Paid holiday taken from the employee's annual entitlement.
v SICK: Absence caused by illness or injury.
v PARENTAL: Leave to care for a new child.
v UNPAID: Absence without pay, agreed with the manager.
v COMPENSATORY: Time off given in return for extra hours already worked.
v OTHER: Any other approved absence, such as study or bereavement leave.
a startDate
s: The first calendar day on which the employee asks to be absent.
u: Entered by the employee; managers approve against team cover, and attendance and payroll begin counting absence from this date.
a requestedUnits
s: How much leave is requested, measured in the organisation's governed unit such as days or hours.
u: Entered or computed from the dates; entitlement checks compare it with the available balance and payroll uses the approved quantity.
a status
s: Where the request is in the approval workflow, from drafting to a final decision.
u: Moved by the employee and the approving manager; only APPROVED requests reduce entitlement and affect attendance or pay.
v DRAFT: Being prepared by the employee and not yet visible to the approver.
v SUBMITTED: Sent to the manager and waiting for a decision.
v APPROVED: Granted by the manager. A final state.
v REJECTED: Refused by the manager. A final state.
v CANCELLED: Withdrawn by the employee. A final state.
