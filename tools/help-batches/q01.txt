@ AIEvaluation
a evaluationType
s: Which quality dimension this evaluation tests: accuracy, fairness, robustness, safety or drift.
u: Chosen by the model risk team when the evaluation is planned.
c: Several types are usually needed before a model is approved for use.
v PERFORMANCE: Measures accuracy and other quality metrics against a labelled test set.
v FAIRNESS: Compares outcomes between groups of people to expose unequal treatment.
v ROBUSTNESS: Tests behaviour on unusual, noisy or adversarial inputs.
v SAFETY: Looks for harmful, offensive or otherwise unacceptable outputs.
v DRIFT: Checks how far performance has moved since the model was deployed.
a results
s: The full set of metrics and observations the tests produced, in the evaluator's own words and figures.
u: Recorded once the tests finish, so reviewers can read the evidence behind the headline metric.
c: Read against the acceptance criteria that the evaluation was planned to meet.
a primaryMetric
s: The headline measure chosen to judge this evaluation, such as AUC, F1 score or error rate.
u: Named by the evaluator and shown in model lists so users can compare evaluations.
c: Always read together with the value recorded beside it.
a primaryMetricValue
s: The number the model achieved on the headline metric during this evaluation.
u: Entered when results are recorded; shown in model lists and trend charts across successive evaluations.
c: Expressed in the scale of the named metric, for example a fraction between zero and one.
a evaluatedAt
s: The date and time at which the evaluation finished and its results were final.
u: Stamped on completion by the evaluator and used to order evaluations and judge how recent the evidence is.
c: Empty until the evaluation has completed.
r reviewedBy
s: The person who independently examined the evaluation and its results.
u: Recorded when the reviewer accepts or challenges the findings, ahead of any model approval.
n: At most one reviewer is named per evaluation; it stays empty until review happens.
w: Gives the model approval step a named, accountable sign-off on the evidence, independent of the model's developers.

@ AIPolicy
a name
s: The title by which the policy is known to governance staff and model owners, for example Customer Data Use Policy.
u: Shown wherever a policy is chosen or attached to a model, so it should be distinctive.
a effectiveFrom
s: The first day on which the policy is binding on the models it covers.
u: Set when the policy is approved, so teams know from which date to comply.
c: Never later than the date the policy stops applying.
a effectiveTo
s: The last day on which the policy still applies to the models it covers.
u: Filled in when a newer policy replaces it or it is withdrawn.
c: Left empty for as long as the policy remains in force.

@ AIPrediction
a overrideReason
s: The explanation a human reviewer gives for rejecting or changing the model's prediction.
u: Written at the moment of overriding and analysed during model monitoring to find systematic weaknesses.
c: Present only on predictions that a person has overridden.

@ Agreement
a status
v DRAFT: Still being written and not yet sent to the other party.
v IN_NEGOTIATION: Shared with the other party and under discussion.
v SIGNED: Signed by every party but not yet effective.
v IN_FORCE: Effective and legally binding on the parties.
v EXPIRED: Reached the end of its term without being renewed.
v TERMINATED: Ended early by one or both parties.
v CANCELLED: Abandoned before anyone signed it.

@ Aircraft
a yearOfManufacture
s: The calendar year in which the manufacturer completed building the aircraft.
u: Copied from the certificate of airworthiness and used in fleet planning, retirement forecasts and insurance premiums.
c: A four-digit year.
a maxTakeoffWeightKg
s: The heaviest weight in kilograms at which the aircraft is certified to begin its take-off roll.
u: Taken from the certificate and used in performance calculations, payload planning and landing-fee charges.
c: Must be greater than zero.

@ Allergy
a category
s: The broad class of substance that triggers the reaction.
u: Chosen by the clinician when recording the allergy; it decides which safety checks the system applies.
c: Medication allergies are checked against every prescription written for the patient.
v MEDICATION: A medicine or one of its ingredients.
v FOOD: A food item or food ingredient.
v ENVIRONMENT: Pollen, dust, animals or other substances in the surroundings.
v BIOLOGIC: A vaccine, blood product or other biological preparation.
a allergyType
v ALLERGY: A true immune-system reaction to the substance.
v INTOLERANCE: A non-immune reaction, such as stomach upset.
a severity
s: How serious the patient's reaction to the substance has been or is expected to be.
u: Set by the clinician and used to decide how strongly the system warns at ordering time.
c: Severe reactions block an order unless a clinician explicitly overrides the warning.
v MILD: Uncomfortable but not dangerous to the patient.
v MODERATE: A reaction that needs treatment but is not life-threatening.
v SEVERE: A life-threatening reaction that must always be avoided.
a onsetDate
s: The date the patient first showed this reaction.
u: Entered by the clinician when it is known; it helps judge how long the allergy has existed.
c: An approximate date is acceptable when the exact day is unknown.
a recordedDate
s: The date the allergy was entered into the patient's record.
u: Set automatically when the clinician records it and kept for the clinical audit trail.
c: Cannot be in the future.

@ AssetAcquisition
a acquisitionNumber
s: The reference number that identifies this acquisition on screens, invoices and audit reports.
u: Assigned by the system when the acquisition is first recorded and never changed afterwards.
c: Unique across all acquisitions.
a inServiceDate
s: The day the asset was first installed and ready to be used in operations.
u: Entered by the asset accountant; depreciation of the asset begins from this date.
c: Required before the acquisition can be capitalised.
a capitalizedAt
s: The date and time the acquisition was posted to the accounts as a fixed asset.
u: Stamped automatically when the accountant capitalises the acquisition.
c: Empty until the acquisition has been capitalised.
a status
s: Whether the acquisition has been posted to the accounts, cancelled or reversed.
u: Moves on as the accountant capitalises the acquisition or corrects it.
c: Only capitalised acquisitions are depreciated.
v RECORDED: Entered and waiting to be capitalised.
v CAPITALIZED: Posted to the accounts, so the asset is now depreciating.
v CANCELLED: Entered in error and withdrawn before capitalisation.
v REVERSED: Capitalised earlier and then reversed by a correcting entry.

@ AssetDisposal
a disposalNumber
s: The reference number that identifies this disposal on screens, approvals and audit reports.
u: Assigned by the system when the disposal is requested and never changed afterwards.
c: Unique across all disposals.
a disposalMethod
v SALE: Sold to an outside buyer for a price.
v SCRAP: Scrapped or recycled with no resale.
v DONATION: Given away free to another party or charity.
v TRADE_IN: Handed in as part payment for a replacement.
v LOSS_OR_THEFT: Lost, stolen or destroyed and written off.
v TRANSFER_OUT: Transferred to another company in the group.
a disposalDate
s: The day the asset actually left the business.
u: Entered when the disposal is completed; depreciation of the asset stops on this date.
c: Required before the disposal can be completed.
a reason
s: A short business justification for retiring the asset, for example replaced by a newer model.
u: Written by the requester and read by the approver when deciding whether to authorise.
c: Free text.
a proceeds
s: The amount of money the business received in return for the asset.
u: Recorded for sales and trade-ins, and used to calculate the gain or loss on disposal.
c: Zero for scrapping, donations and losses.
a approvedAt
s: The date and time the approver authorised the disposal.
u: Stamped automatically when the approver accepts the request.
c: Required before the disposal can be completed.
a status
s: Where the disposal stands between request, approval and completion.
u: Moves on as the approver decides and the asset is physically removed.
c: Only completed disposals take the asset off the register.
v REQUESTED: Asked for and waiting for an approver.
v APPROVED: Approved, so the asset may now be removed.
v COMPLETED: The asset has gone and the disposal is posted.
v REJECTED: Refused by the approver, so the asset stays.
v CANCELLED: Withdrawn by the requester before completion.
r approvedBy
s: The person who authorised the asset to be disposed of.
u: Recorded when the approver accepts the request.
n: At most one approver is named per disposal; it stays empty until approval.
w: Provides the sign-off that lets a disposal move on to completion, and must come from someone with authority for the asset's value.

@ AssetTransfer
a transferNumber
s: The reference number that identifies this transfer on screens, shipping documents and audit reports.
u: Assigned by the system when the transfer is requested and never changed afterwards.
c: Unique across all transfers.
a reason
s: A short explanation of why the asset is changing location, owner or custodian.
u: Written by the requester and read by the approver and the receiving site.
c: Free text, for example site closure or staff relocation.
a shippedAt
s: The date and time the asset left its previous location.
u: Recorded by the sending site for physical moves.
c: Present once the asset is in transit.
a receivedAt
s: The date and time the receiving site confirmed the asset had arrived.
u: Recorded when the receiver acknowledges delivery.
c: Present once the transfer is completed.
a status
s: Where the transfer stands between request, shipment and confirmation of arrival.
u: Moves on as the sender ships the asset and the receiver confirms it.
c: The asset register changes only when the transfer completes.
v REQUESTED: Asked for, but the asset has not moved yet.
v IN_TRANSIT: The asset has left and has not yet arrived.
v COMPLETED: The receiver confirmed arrival and the register is updated.
v CANCELLED: Called off before the move was completed.

@ BankLoan

c: Bullet loans are repaid in one payment at maturity.

@ BankReconciliation
a reconciliationNumber
s: The reference number that identifies this reconciliation in the finance team's records.
u: Assigned by the system when the reconciliation is opened and cited in month-end checklists.
c: Unique across all reconciliations.
a periodStart
s: The first day of the statement period being matched against the books.
u: Set by the preparer when opening the reconciliation, alongside the period end.
c: Not later than the period end.
a statementBalance
s: The closing balance printed on the bank's statement for the period.
u: Copied by the preparer from the statement and compared with the book balance to find differences.
c: Held in the bank account's currency.
a reconciledAt
s: The date and time the reviewer signed off that the books agree with the bank.
u: Stamped automatically at sign-off.
c: Present once the reconciliation is reconciled.
a status
s: Whether the matching is in progress, awaiting review or signed off.
u: Moves on as the preparer submits the work and the reviewer signs it off.
c: Reconciled periods are locked against further change.
v OPEN: Matching of transactions is still in progress.
v IN_REVIEW: Submitted to the reviewer for sign-off.
v RECONCILED: Signed off, so the books agree with the bank.
r preparedBy
s: The finance person who did the matching and compiled the reconciliation.
u: Recorded when the reconciliation is submitted for review.
n: Exactly one preparer is named; it stays empty until submission.
w: Names who did the work, so segregation of duties can require a different person to review it.
r reviewedBy
s: The finance person who checked the reconciliation and signed it off.
u: Recorded at sign-off, when the status becomes reconciled.
n: At most one reviewer is named; it stays empty until sign-off.
w: Supplies the independent approval that closes the reconciliation and locks the period.
