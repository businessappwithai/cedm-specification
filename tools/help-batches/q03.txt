@ CostCenter
a category
v PRODUCTION: Makes products or delivers services, so its costs flow into product cost.
v SALES: Sells and markets the company's offering; its costs are reported as selling expense.
v ADMINISTRATION: Runs general administration such as finance, HR and management; its costs stay out of product cost.
v RESEARCH: Researches and develops new products and methods; its costs are reported as research and development expense.
v SERVICE: Provides internal services to other cost centres, such as IT or maintenance, and is usually allocated onward.
a status
s: Whether postings may currently be made to the cost centre.
u: Moved on by controlling when a unit is paused or retired; budget owners check it before charging costs.
c: Blocked and closed cost centres are refused on new postings, while their earlier postings stay in reports.
v ACTIVE: Open for postings and available when costs are assigned.
v BLOCKED: Temporarily closed for postings, for example during a reorganisation or review.
v CLOSED: No longer used for new postings; kept so past costs can still be reported.
@ Credit
a grantedDate
s: The day the credit was granted to the customer or account.
u: Recorded by whoever grants the credit; the expiry period and any approval limit are counted from this date.
c: Not later than the expiry date.
a expiryDate
s: The last day the credit can still be applied to a charge.
u: Credit left unused after this day lapses and can no longer be applied to invoices or charges.
c: Left empty when the credit never expires.
r grantedBy
s: The person who decided to grant the credit.
u: Recorded when the credit is granted, so a reviewer can see who authorised it and why.
n: At most one person is recorded as granting each credit.
w: Credits above an approval limit are checked against the grantor's authority before they take effect.
@ Crop
a scientificName
s: The botanical name of the crop, such as Triticum aestivum for bread wheat.
u: Used in seed orders, certificates and regulatory records where a common name would be ambiguous.
c: Written as genus and species.
a variety
s: The particular variety or cultivar of the crop that is grown, such as a named wheat strain.
u: Recorded so yields, treatments and seed lots can be traced and compared between varieties.
c: Spelled as on the seed certificate.
a growingDays
s: The typical number of days from sowing to harvest for this crop.
u: Agronomists use it to plan sowing windows, field rotation and expected harvest dates.
c: Varies with climate and season, so treat it as a planning average.
@ CustomsDeclaration
a dutyAmount
s: The total customs duty and import taxes assessed on the declared goods.
u: Entered from the customs authority's assessment, paid to customs and booked into the landed cost of the goods.
c: Stated in the local currency of the declaring country.
@ DataQualityAssessment
a assessedAt
s: The date and time at which the assessment ran against the dataset.
u: Recorded when the assessment completes; quality trends for the dataset are plotted along it.
c: Present once the assessment has completed.
a recordsAssessed
s: The number of records in the dataset that were examined during this assessment.
u: Filled in when the run completes, so readers know how much data each score is based on.
c: The percentage scores are measured against this count.
a completenessPercent
s: The percentage of required values that were actually present in the records checked.
u: Calculated by the completeness rules of the assessment and shown beside the other quality dimensions.
c: A value between 0 and 100.
a uniquenessPercent
s: The percentage of records checked that were not duplicates of another record.
u: Calculated by the matching rules of the assessment; a low figure points to records that need merging.
c: A value between 0 and 100.
a overallScore
s: The single combined quality score for the dataset in this assessment.
u: Weighted from the individual dimension scores and shown in data catalogues so consumers can judge trust at a glance.
c: A value between 0 and 100.
a meetsTarget
s: Whether every quality dimension reached the target set for the dataset.
u: Worked out when the assessment completes; a false result causes data quality issues to be raised.
c: Present once the assessment has completed.
a issuesSummary
s: A short narrative of what was wrong with the data in this assessment.
u: Written for the data owner, who reads it to decide what to clean up first.
c: Detailed lists of failing records are attached separately.
@ Dataset
a recordCount
s: How many records the dataset currently holds.
u: Refreshed whenever the dataset is reloaded, and shown in the catalogue so users can judge its size.
c: Only approximate for very large or streaming datasets.
a containsPersonalData
s: Whether the dataset holds personal data about identifiable people.
u: Answered by the data owner; a yes brings data-protection duties such as access limits and retention rules.
c: Set by the data owner and reviewed when the contents change.
@ Delivery
a deliveredAt
s: The date and time the goods were actually handed over to the recipient.
u: Taken from the signed proof of delivery rather than typed in by hand.
c: The legal delivery time used for invoicing and claims.
a outcome
s: How the handover went, as recorded on the proof of delivery.
u: Set from the proof of delivery by the carrier or driver, and read by customer service when something went wrong.
c: Any outcome other than a clean delivery opens a claim or a follow-up with the customer.
v DELIVERED: Handed over complete and in good condition.
v DELIVERED_WITH_EXCEPTION: Handed over but short, over or damaged, so a claim or note is needed.
v REFUSED: The recipient declined to accept the goods and they return to the sender.
a packagesDelivered
s: The number of packages or pallets actually handed over at the destination.
u: Counted by the driver at handover and compared with the shipment's expected count to spot shortages.
c: A difference from the shipment count is treated as an exception.
@ DeliveryAttempt
a attemptedAt
s: The date and time this attempt to deliver the payload was made.
u: Stamped by the integration layer when the attempt starts, so support staff can reconstruct the order of events.
c: A message's attempts are listed in this order.
a result
s: What came of this attempt to deliver the payload to the endpoint.
u: Set from the endpoint's response; operators filter on it when diagnosing a delivery problem.
c: Rejected messages are not retried, while failed and timed-out ones are tried again.
v SUCCESS: The endpoint accepted the message and no further attempt is needed.
v REJECTED: The endpoint refused the message as invalid, so retrying will not help.
v FAILED: The endpoint could not be reached or answered with an error, so the message can be retried.
v TIMEOUT: The endpoint did not answer in time, so the outcome is unknown and a retry is allowed.
a responseMessage
s: The text the endpoint sent back in answer to this attempt.
u: Read by operators diagnosing why a delivery failed or was rejected.
c: Can be long and is kept exactly as returned.
a durationMs
s: How long the attempt took from sending to answer, in milliseconds.
u: Used to spot slow or degrading endpoints before they start timing out.
c: Measured by the integration layer, not entered by a person.
@ Department
a description
s: What the department is responsible for and what kind of work it does.
u: Read by people placing staff, positions or work, so they choose the right department.
c: Free text written by the organisation's administrators.
a status
s: Whether the department currently exists as part of the organisation.
u: Set to inactive after a reorganisation closes or merges the department.
c: Inactive departments are not offered when assigning people or work.
v ACTIVE: The department exists and can receive people, positions and work.
v INACTIVE: The department has been closed or merged away; it stays on record for history.
@ Deposit
a dayCountBasis
s: The convention used to count days when interest is calculated.
u: Taken from the bank's terms when the deposit is arranged, so expected interest matches the bank's own figure.
c: The same rate earns slightly more on actual/360 than on actual/365.
v ACT_360: Actual days elapsed divided by a 360-day year.
v ACT_365: Actual days elapsed divided by a 365-day year.
v THIRTY_360: Every month counted as 30 days, divided by a 360-day year.
a interestReceived
s: The interest the bank actually paid on the deposit.
u: Recorded by treasury at maturity or when the deposit is broken early.
c: Differences from the expected interest are investigated with the bank.
a status
s: Where the deposit stands, from arrangement through to repayment.
u: Moved on by treasury as the money is placed, matures or is withdrawn early.
c: Only active deposits count against the limits set for each bank.
v ARRANGED: Agreed with the bank but not yet placed.
v ACTIVE: Placed and earning interest.
v MATURED: Repaid at maturity together with its interest.
v BROKEN: Repaid early, usually with reduced interest or a fee.
v CANCELLED: Called off before the money was placed.
@ Diagnosis
a clinicalStatus
s: Whether the condition is currently affecting the patient.
u: Updated by clinicians as the condition changes over time.
c: Resolved conditions stay on the patient's history.
v ACTIVE: The condition is present and being managed.
v REMISSION: The condition is present but not currently causing problems.
v RESOLVED: The condition is over and no longer treated.
v ENTERED_IN_ERROR: Recorded by mistake, so it should be disregarded and not counted as a real condition.
a resolvedDate
s: The date on which the condition was judged to be over.
u: Recorded by the clinician when the clinical status becomes resolved.
c: Not earlier than the onset date.
@ Dock
a name
s: A descriptive name for the dock, such as "North yard door 7".
u: Shown in dock schedules and to drivers directed to the door.
c: Optional, since the code already identifies the door.
a status
v AVAILABLE: In service and offered when appointments and loads are scheduled.
v OUT_OF_SERVICE: Not usable, for example during repair, and left out of scheduling.
@ DocumentClassification
a sensitivity
v PUBLIC: May be shared outside the company.
v INTERNAL: For staff only and not to be shared outside the company.
v CONFIDENTIAL: For the people who need the documents for their work.
v RESTRICTED: For named people only, because disclosure would do serious harm.
a retentionTrigger
s: The event from which the retention period is counted.
u: Taken from the retention schedule, and read together with the number of years to work out the disposal date.
c: A document is kept until this event plus the retention years.
v CREATION: The day the document was created.
v FISCAL_YEAR_END: The end of the fiscal year the document belongs to.
v CONTRACT_END: The end of the contract the document belongs to.
v EMPLOYMENT_END: The day the employee the document concerns left.
v CASE_CLOSURE: The closing of the case the document belongs to.
a disposalAction
v DESTROY: Destroyed securely once the retention period has run.
v ARCHIVE: Moved to a permanent archive.
v REVIEW: Reviewed by a person who decides what happens next.
a status
v DRAFT: Being prepared and not yet offered for filing.
v ACTIVE: In use and offered when documents are filed.
v RETIRED: No longer used for new documents.
@ DocumentVersion
a storageLocation
s: The place in document storage where this version's file is held.
u: Set by the system when the version is checked in, so the right file is retrieved when it is opened.
c: Users never type it.
a releasedAt
s: The date and time the version was approved and released.
u: Recorded automatically on approval and used to order versions and to show which was current at any moment.
c: Present once the version has been released.
a status
s: Where the version stands in its review and release.
u: Moved on by the review and release steps as the author submits and an approver signs off.
c: Only one version of a document is released at a time.
v DRAFT: Being written and not yet sent for approval.
v IN_REVIEW: Sent to approvers and waiting for a decision.
v RELEASED: Approved and the current version people should use.
v SUPERSEDED: Replaced by a later released version.
v WITHDRAWN: Abandoned before it was ever released.
r author
s: The person who wrote this version of the document.
u: Recorded on check-in, so readers know whom to ask about its content.
n: One author is recorded for each version.
w: The author submits the version for review but should not be the one who approves it.
r approvedBy
s: The person who approved the version for release.
u: Recorded when the approver signs off, as evidence of who authorised the release.
n: One approver is recorded for each version.
w: Approval moves the version out of review; the approver must hold authority for the document's class.

c: Free text written by the academic department.
a prerequisites
s: The courses a student must have passed before taking this one.
u: Checked when a student enrols, so people without the background are stopped or warned.

a requiredCredits
s: The number of credits a student must earn to complete the programme.

@ Employee
a jobTitle
s: The title that describes the employee's role, such as Senior Accountant.
u: Shown in directories, on documents and in organisation charts.
c: Usually the title of the position they hold.
a workEmail
s: The email address the employee uses for work.
u: Used for system notifications, approvals and the staff directory.
c: Usually also the employee's user name for signing in.
@ Employment
a weeklyHours
s: The number of hours per week the employee is contracted to work.
u: Used by payroll, leave calculation and capacity planning, and set from the employment contract.
c: Full-time hours depend on the country.
a payBasis
s: The way the employee's pay is worked out.
u: Decides how payroll computes pay each period and what records it needs.
c: Hourly employees need time records before they can be paid.
v SALARIED: Paid a fixed salary per pay period regardless of hours.
v HOURLY: Paid for the hours worked, based on time records.
v COMMISSION: Paid mainly as a share of the sales made.
@ EnergyAsset
a commissionedDate
s: The date the asset was put into service.
u: Starts the asset's operating history and is used to calculate age and plan maintenance.
c: Not in the future.
@ EnergyTariff
a unitRate
s: The price charged for each unit of energy consumed.
u: Multiplied by the metered consumption to compute the energy charge on the bill.
c: Per the tariff's unit of measure; where there are time bands it is the peak rate.
a standingCharge
s: The fixed charge applied each billing period, whatever the consumption.
u: Added to every bill for the customer on this tariff.
c: Per month unless the tariff states otherwise.

u: Chosen at enrolment and used to decide fees, study load and expected duration.
c: May change during the programme.
v FULL_TIME: A full study load, completing in the programme's standard duration.
v PART_TIME: A reduced study load, so the programme takes longer.
v DISTANCE: Studying remotely rather than attending on campus.
a expectedCompletionDate
s: The date the student is expected to finish the programme.
u: Calculated at enrolment and used for planning and progression reports.
c: Depends on the programme length and the student's study mode.
a completionDate
s: The date the student finished the enrolment or left it.
u: Recorded when the enrolment ends, whether by completing or withdrawing.
c: Present only once the enrolment is completed or withdrawn.
a withdrawalReason

u: Recorded by the registrar at withdrawal and used in retention analysis.
c: Present only on withdrawn enrolments.

a serialNumber

u: Quoted for warranty claims and checked against recall notices.
c: Copied as it appears on the nameplate.
a installationDate

u: Starts its operating history and its warranty period.
c: Not in the future.
a operatingStatus

u: Updated by operations and maintenance whenever the equipment starts, stops or is retired.
c: Downtime is measured from changes of this status.

v DOWN: Not running because of a failure or maintenance.

@ Escalation

s: The reference number by which the escalation is quoted.
u: Assigned automatically when the escalation is raised and used in messages and reports.
c: Unique across all escalations.
a reason

u: Chosen when raising the escalation; escalation analysis groups by it.
c: Read together with the free-text description.

u: Recorded on acknowledgement and compared with the target response time.
c: Present once the escalation has been acknowledged.
a resolvedAt
s: The date and time the escalation was resolved.
u: Recorded together with the resolution, and used to measure how long escalations stay open.
c: Present once the escalation has been resolved.
@ Experiment

s: What the experiment showed, written up in the researcher's own words.
u: Written on completion and read in project reviews when deciding what to do next.
c: The raw data is attached separately.
@ Facility
a capacity
s: How many people the facility is designed to hold.
u: Used for occupancy planning and for deciding where teams and events can be placed.

@ Failure
a failureNumber
s: The reference number by which the failure is quoted.
u: Assigned automatically when the failure is reported and used on repair orders and reports.
c: Unique across all failures.
a cause
s: Why the equipment failed, such as "lubrication missed".
u: Recorded after investigation and required before the failure can be closed.
c: Recurring causes are addressed by changing maintenance plans.
a severity
s: How much the failure mattered to operations.
u: Set when the failure is reported and used to decide the repair priority.
c: Read together with the equipment's criticality.
v MINOR: Degraded performance, but production continues.
v MAJOR: Production on the equipment stopped.
v CRITICAL: Safety, the environment or a whole production line was affected.
a startedAt
s: The date and time the failure began.
u: Given by the reporter, and downtime is measured from this moment.
c: Not later than the end time.
a endedAt
s: The date and time the equipment was running again.
u: Recorded by the technician when the repair is finished and used to total the downtime.
c: Present once the equipment is repaired.
a status
s: Where the failure stands from report to closure.
u: Moved on by maintenance as the failure is picked up, repaired and closed.
c: Closed failures have a cause recorded.
v REPORTED: Reported and not yet worked on.
v UNDER_REPAIR: A technician is repairing it now.
v REPAIRED: The equipment runs again.
v CLOSED: Cause recorded and nothing more to do.
v CANCELLED: Turned out not to be a failure after all.
r reportedBy
s: The person who reported the failure.
u: Recorded with the report so maintenance can ask them for details.
n: One reporter is recorded for each failure.
w: The reporter starts the repair process and is usually an operator.
